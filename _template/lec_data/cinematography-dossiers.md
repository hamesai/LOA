# Cinematography lecturers: dossiers (Lecture Mode, 12/10/2026)

Built under the summon skill's Lecture Mode. Voiced lines in the course are simulated, written for teaching, and are not quotations. They add no facts beyond these dossiers and the lessons. Confidence varies and was upgraded on 12/10/2026 after reading primary texts: Toland rich, Freund partial to rich, Muybridge and Eastman partial, Paul faint.

## Eadweard Muybridge (1830 to 1904): partial
- **Sources:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eadweard_Muybridge (opening and 1872 to 1879 sections read).
- **Documented methods:** faster mechanical shutters and electrically triggered mechanisms; more sensitive emulsions for shorter exposures; a battery of 12 cameras at Palo Alto in June 1878 with shutters tripped by wires; 24 cameras in 1879; let reporters inspect the original negative of a retouched 1877 picture; a session on 15 June 1878 in which an accident captured on the negatives convinced sceptics.
- **Voice:** not documented in what we read. Voice in the course is inferred: plain, practical, evidence-minded.
- **Knowledge bounds:** died 1904. Knew still photography, shutters, plates and the zoopraxiscope. Knew nothing of digital sensors or modern film stocks.
- **Gaps and disputes:** the original 1877 negative has not resurfaced. His own writings were not read.
- **Used in:** module 1 and the capstone.

## Gregg Toland (1904 to 1948): rich
- **Sources:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregg_Toland (opening, Citizen Kane and deep focus sections read).
- **Documented methods:** closed the aperture to f/8 to f/16 when most productions used f/2.3 to f/3.5; used brighter carbon arc lights, coated lenses of his own design made with Caltech engineers (Vard Opticoat), and Kodak Super XX (ASA 100); modified the Mitchell camera for wider movement and low angles; devised a remote focus control; collaborated on composite shots with Linwood G. Dunn.
- **Voice:** one quoted tribute from Welles; his own voice is not documented in what we read. Voice in the course is inferred: technical, collaborative.
- **Knowledge bounds:** died 1948. Knew film, arc lights, optical printers and Technicolor of his time. Knew nothing of DSLRs.
- **Gaps and disputes:** historians disagree on whether Toland or Welles deserves more credit for the look of Citizen Kane. The page has "citation needed" marks on some passages.
- **Used in:** modules 2 and 3.

## Karl Freund (1890 to 1969): partial to rich
- **Sources:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Freund (opening, unchained camera, television and Photo Research sections read).
- **Documented methods:** the unchained camera (off the tripod, worn on the stomach, on a cart on a track, on a crane); the three-camera set-up for I Love Lucy with a centre wide camera and two close-up cameras 75 to 90 degrees away; flat lighting that lets cameras move without changing the light; Photo Research Corporation, the Norwood Director incident light meter and a brightness meter, two Academy Awards for technical achievement.
- **Voice:** not documented in what we read. Inferred: practical, inventive.
- **Knowledge bounds:** died 1969. Knew black-and-white film, television in front of a live audience and analog meters.
- **Gaps and disputes:** the page gives two founding dates for Photo Research (1941 and 1944).
- **Used in:** modules 4 and 5.

## George Eastman (1854 to 1932): partial
- **Sources:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Eastman (opening and founding of Kodak sections read).
- **Documented methods:** roll film; the 1888 Kodak camera sold loaded with film for 100 exposures and mailed back with $10 for processing, prints and a reload; separation of taking from developing; focus on film as the main revenue.
- **Voice:** one slogan, "You press the button, we do the rest", and a suicide note quoted on the page. The course uses neither beyond the idea. Inferred voice: businesslike.
- **Knowledge bounds:** died 1932. Knew film, dry plates and early colour. Knew nothing of digital capture.
- **Gaps and disputes:** the page notes that he controlled his public image and that a definitive biography only appeared in 1996. His views and donations are outside what the course needs.
- **Used in:** module 6.

## Robert W. Paul (1869 to 1943): faint
- **Sources:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_W._Paul (opening and film career sections read).
- **Documented methods:** reverse-engineered the Kinetoscope; built his own camera when only copies of Edison films existed; the Cinematograph Camera No. 1 (April 1896) with reverse-cranking for multiple exposures; Scrooge, or, Marley's Ghost (1901); Britain's first film studio (1898).
- **Voice:** one quoted patent passage; otherwise not documented. Inferred: inventive, instrument-maker's precision.
- **Knowledge bounds:** died 1943. Knew mechanical film cameras and projectors.
- **Gaps and disputes:** the page notes that credit for some firsts is claimed for others, such as Louis Le Prince.
- **Used in:** module 7.

## Primary sources added 12/10/2026
- **Toland:** Realism for Citizen Kane, American Cinematographer, February 1941, read in full at https://theasc.com/article/realism-for-citizen-kane. Facts used: f/8 or smaller, often f/11, once f/16, against f/2.3 to f/2.8; Opticote coating worth half to one stop; arc broadsides from the floor; much lower lighting contrast; 24 to 35 mm lenses near universal focus at f/11 to f/16; test each lens. His note gives Super XX a Weston rating of 64, against ASA 100 on Wikipedia.
- **Freund:** his account of filming I Love Lucy, read in full as reproduced at https://eyesofageneration.com/the-man-who-filmed-i-love-lucy-tells-us-how-it-was-done-by-karl-freund/ (the original print was not seen). Facts used: overhead lights, lower subject contrast, three BNC Mitchells with T-stop lenses, 28 to 50 mm centre and 3 to 4 inch close-up lenses 75 to 90 degrees apart, crews of four, four days to line up a show. Not reachable: lucyfan.com copy.
- **Muybridge:** Animal Locomotion prospectus (1887), opening excerpts read at https://archive.org/stream/animallocomotion00muyb/animallocomotion00muyb_djvu.txt. The full text was not fetched.
- **Eastman:** US Patent 388,850 read in part at https://patents.google.com/patent/US388850A/en.
- **Paul:** no primary source found; still one Wikipedia page.
