# Lecturers: sources and verification

Biography lines checked on 11/10/2026 by web search against the sources below. 'checked' means the headline fact was found in that source.

Each lecturer's method lesson (the last lesson of the module, or the one before the capstone's business lens) cites its own numbered sources, shown in the module's Sources list and kept in `_template/lec_data/_refs.json`. Lecturer's notes and welcomes are simulated voices written for teaching: they are not quotations.

Source quality: primary texts were read where available (Mendel 1865, Jekyll, Nightingale, Kephart 1910, Shackleton's South, Snow 1855, Franklin 1735, Rogers 1957 abstract and text, Churchill 1897, Douglass 1852 excerpts, Aristotle Book II). Several lessons rest on secondary sources, and each says so in a callout: Carson (Britannica and a study-guide summary, not the full book), Ziglar (a book summary), the Cadbury cocoa case (largely the family's own account, plus a Springer chapter abstract and a JSTOR abstract), the DeLorme set scheme (a trainer's article quoting the original papers), Rumford's principles (a Victorian summary).

Not source-checked: birth and death dates for Liebig, Snow, DeLorme, Quintilian, Douglass, Aristotle and Cicero (standard dates). Joseph Paxton: Britannica gives 1801 as birth year, Wikipedia gives 1803; the course uses 1803.

The Wasteland Kitchen business lenses use the owner's Google Doc 'Fallout Food Preperation' (Fallout Pantry tab and the Fallout Food Prep tab, read 11/10/2026) as the worked case. Its figures are the owner's and were not independently checked. UK rules come from GOV.UK and Food Standards Agency pages dated 2023 to 2026.

## Biography lines and sources

- **Joseph Paxton** (1803–1865). Gardener at Chatsworth who built glasshouses there, then designed the Crystal Palace for the 1851 Great Exhibition. Source: https://www.britannica.com/biography/Joseph-Paxton. Status: checked (Britannica gives birth year as 1801; Wikipedia gives 1803).
- **Gertrude Jekyll** (1843–1932). Garden designer who worked with the architect Edwin Lutyens and was known for her handling of colour. Source: https://www.britannica.com/biography/Gertrude-Jekyll. Status: checked.
- **Gregor Mendel** (1822–1884). Augustinian friar and abbot whose pea-plant experiments (1856–1863) established the rules of heredity. Source: https://www.britannica.com/biography/Gregor-Mendel. Status: checked.
- **Justus von Liebig** (1803–1873). Chemist who popularised the law of the minimum, that growth is limited by the scarcest resource. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liebig%27s_law_of_the_minimum. Status: law checked; dates not checked this session.
- **Rachel Carson** (1907–1964). Marine biologist and writer whose book Silent Spring (1962) raised public concern about pesticides. Source: https://www.britannica.com/biography/Rachel-Carson. Status: checked (Britannica, 11/10/2026).
- **John Muir** (1838–1914). Scottish-born American naturalist who founded the Sierra Club in 1892 and campaigned for national parks. Source: https://www.britannica.com/biography/John-Muir. Status: checked.
- **Horace Kephart** (1862–1931). American writer and librarian, author of the outdoors guide Camping and Woodcraft (1906). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horace_Kephart. Status: checked.
- **Fridtjof Nansen** (1861–1930). Norwegian explorer who led the first crossing of Greenland on skis (1888) and the Fram expedition (1893–1896). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fridtjof_Nansen. Status: checked.
- **Florence Nightingale** (1820–1910). Nurse and statistician who pressed for sanitary reform and presented mortality data in her polar area (rose) diagram. Source: https://www.history.com/topics/womens-history/florence-nightingale. Status: checked.
- **Ernest Shackleton** (1874–1922). Anglo-Irish polar explorer who led the Endurance expedition (1914–1916), after which none of the Endurance crew had died. Source: https://www.britannica.com/biography/Ernest-Henry-Shackleton. Status: checked.
- **Louis Pasteur** (1822–1895). French chemist and microbiologist who developed the germ theory of disease and pasteurization. Source: https://www.britannica.com/biography/Louis-Pasteur. Status: checked.
- **John Snow** (1813–1858). London physician who traced the 1854 Broad Street cholera outbreak to a contaminated water pump. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1854_Broad_Street_cholera_outbreak. Status: outbreak checked; dates not checked this session.
- **Count Rumford (Benjamin Thompson)** (1753–1814). American-born physicist who studied heat, designed fuel-saving stoves and fireplaces, and devised Rumford's Soup for the poor in Bavaria. Source: https://www.britannica.com/biography/Sir-Benjamin-Thompson-Graf-von-Rumford. Status: checked.
- **Nicolas Appert** (c.1749–1841). French confectioner who invented preserving food in sealed, heated jars and won a 12,000-franc award in 1810. Source: https://www.britannica.com/biography/Nicolas-Appert. Status: checked.
- **Benjamin Franklin** (1706–1790). Printer, scientist and statesman of colonial and revolutionary America. Source: https://www.britannica.com/biography/Benjamin-Franklin. Status: checked (Britannica, 11/10/2026).
- **Jerry Morris** (1910–2009). British epidemiologist whose 1953 Lancet studies of London bus drivers and conductors linked physical activity to heart disease. Source: https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736(12)61027-7/fulltext. Status: checked.
- **Carl Rogers** (1902–1987). American psychologist who originated the client-centred approach to psychotherapy. Source: https://www.britannica.com/biography/Carl-Rogers. Status: checked.
- **Hans Selye** (1907–1982). Austrian-born endocrinologist who described the general adaptation syndrome to stress in 1936. Source: https://www.britannica.com/biography/Hans-Selye. Status: checked.
- **Thomas DeLorme** (1902–1987). American physician whose 1945 protocol of progressive resistance exercise changed rehabilitation. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_overload. Status: 1945 protocol checked; dates not checked this session.
- **Albert Bandura** (1925–2021). Psychologist who described self-efficacy, a person's belief in their ability to act, in 1977. Source: https://albertbandura.com/albert-bandura-self-efficacy.html. Status: checked.
- **Aristotle** (384–322 BC). Greek philosopher whose Rhetoric names three means of persuasion: character, the hearer's emotion and argument. Source: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/aristotle-rhetoric. Status: checked (dates standard, not checked).
- **Cicero** (106–43 BC). Roman statesman and orator who wrote On the Orator (De oratore, c.55 BC) on the education of the speaker. Source: https://www.britannica.com/topic/De-oratore. Status: checked (dates standard, not checked).
- **Quintilian** (c.35–c.100). Roman teacher of rhetoric who wrote the twelve-book Institutio Oratoria on training an orator. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institutio_Oratoria. Status: book checked; dates not checked this session.
- **Winston Churchill** (1874–1965). British statesman awarded the 1953 Nobel Prize in Literature partly for his oratory. Source: https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/literature/1953/summary. Status: checked.
- **Frederick Douglass** (1818–1895). Abolitionist and writer, thought to have given more than 2,000 speeches, including the 1852 Rochester oration on the Fourth of July. Source: https://www.gilderlehrman.org/history-resources/essays/frederick-douglass-orator. Status: speeches checked; dates not checked this session.
- **John H. Patterson** (1844–1922). Founder of National Cash Register, who gave each salesman an exclusive territory and opened a sales training school. Source: https://www.britannica.com/money/John-Henry-Patterson. Status: checked.
- **Dale Carnegie** (1888–1955). American teacher of public speaking whose How to Win Friends and Influence People appeared in 1936. Source: https://www.dalecarnegie.com/en/books/how-to-win-friends-influence-people. Status: checked.
- **Harry Gordon Selfridge** (1858–1947). American-born retailer who opened Selfridges on Oxford Street on 15 March 1909. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Gordon_Selfridge. Status: checked.
- **Zig Ziglar** (1926–2012). American salesman, author and motivational speaker. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zig_Ziglar. Status: checked.
- **George Cadbury** (1839–1922). Quaker chocolate manufacturer who built the Bournville model village from 1893 and improved working conditions. Source: https://www.britannica.com/money/George-Cadbury. Status: checked.
- **Konstantin Stanislavski** (1863–1938). Russian actor and director who co-founded the Moscow Art Theatre in 1898 and developed the Stanislavsky system of acting. Source: https://www.britannica.com/biography/Konstantin-Stanislavsky. Status: checked (Britannica spells the name Stanislavsky).
- **Harley Granville-Barker** (1877–1946). English actor, director and playwright who staged Shakespeare at the Savoy in 1912–14 and wrote the Prefaces to Shakespeare. Source: https://www.britannica.com/biography/Harley-Granville-Barker. Status: checked.
- **Vsevolod Meyerhold** (1874–1940). Russian director and actor whose non-realistic theatre, including biomechanics, made him a seminal force in modern theatre. Source: https://www.britannica.com/biography/Vsevolod-Yemilyevich-Meyerhold. Status: checked (Russian sources give the death year as 1940 or 1942).
- **Sergei Eisenstein** (1898–1948). Russian film director and theorist of montage, maker of Battleship Potemkin (1925). Source: https://www.britannica.com/biography/Sergey-Eisenstein. Status: checked.
- **Peter Brook** (1925–2022). English director of the 1970 RSC A Midsummer Night's Dream and author of The Empty Space (1968). Source: https://www.britannica.com/biography/Peter-Brook. Status: checked.

- **Josiah Wedgwood** (1730–1795). English potter who founded the Wedgwood company in 1759. Sources: https://www.britannica.com/biography/Josiah-Wedgwood and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josiah_Wedgwood. Status: checked. Only the opening statement of McKendrick's 1970 cost accounting article was read, so the lessons make no claims about its findings.
- **Thomas Chippendale** (1718–1779). English cabinetmaker, author of The Gentleman and Cabinet-Maker's Director (1754). Source: https://www.britannica.com/biography/Thomas-Chippendale. Status: checked (baptised 1718, buried 1779).
- **Arthur Hill Hassall** (1817–1894). British physician, chemist and microscopist; Lancet reports on food adulteration led to the 1860 Act. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Hill_Hassall. Status: checked, Wikipedia only (the book's catalogue page was seen, not the book).
- **Luca Pacioli** (born 1446 to 1448). Italian mathematician and Franciscan friar; Summa de arithmetica, Venice, 1494. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luca_Pacioli. Status: birth range checked; death year not read.

## Cinematography (12/10/2026)

- **Gregg Toland** (1904 to 1948). American cinematographer; deep focus, coated lenses, Super XX. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregg_Toland. Status: Wikipedia excerpts read. Credit for the look of Citizen Kane is disputed and the lesson says so.

- **Robert W. Paul** (1869 to 1943). English film pioneer; reverse-cranking camera, 1896. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_W._Paul. Status: Wikipedia excerpts read.
