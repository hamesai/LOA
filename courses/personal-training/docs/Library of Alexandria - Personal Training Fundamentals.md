## Module 1: The job, the guidelines and the evidence

Prerequisites: None. Time: 3 h.

### Core idea
Personal training sits between public-health guidance and individual coaching. You need the scope of the job, the WHO weekly targets, and the habit of asking how well supported a claim is.

### Tutorial

1. Place a client scenario in coach, adapt-and-check or refer.
2. Convert mixed-intensity minutes to moderate-equivalent and compare with 150 to 300.
3. State the strength and sedentary-time guidance.
4. Judge a claim by population, outcome and certainty.

Worked example: Client does 90 minutes moderate and 30 minutes vigorous: 90 + 2 x 30 = 150 moderate-equivalent minutes, at the bottom of the WHO range. The 2-to-1 trade is our inference.

### Challenges

**C1 (warm-up).** Write a 60-word scope statement for new clients saying what you will coach and what you will refer.
- Answer key: Names assessment, goals, programmes and coaching; names symptoms, diagnosis and medical diets as referrals.
- Self-check: Compare with the three zones in lesson 1: every referral item should be outside scope.

**C2 (core).** A client does 3 x 20 minutes of moderate cycling and 2 x 25 minutes of running (vigorous). Compute moderate-equivalent minutes and say whether they are in the 150 to 300 range.
- Answer key: 60 + 2 x 50 = 160 moderate-equivalent minutes; yes, in range.
- Self-check: Recompute: 3 x 20 = 60; 2 x 25 = 50; 50 x 2 = 100; 60 + 100 = 160.

**C3 (stretch).** Take a social-media fitness claim and write which population, outcome and certainty it would need to be reliable, then rate it.
- Answer key: Names a population, an outcome, a certainty level (high, moderate, low) and a verdict.
- Self-check: Check against the four habits in lesson 4: population, outcome, certainty and stated inference.

### Common mistakes

- Treating the 2-to-1 minute trade as a WHO rule. The WHO gives ranges; the trade is our inference.
- Quoting a pooled average as a promise to an individual. Say 'on average' and note variation.
- Coaching someone with new chest pain. Stop and refer.

### Resources

- [WHO 2020 guidelines on physical activity and sedentary behaviour](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/54/24/1451) (Scholarly, Intermediate, Verified). Read in full (body text). The activity targets and special-population advice.
- [UK Chief Medical Officers' physical activity guidelines](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/physical-activity-guidelines-uk-chief-medical-officers-report) (Reference, Beginner, Verified). Page opened; only the update history was read. Check the current adult wording yourself.
- [CIMSPA professional standards (search)](https://www.google.com/search?q=CIMSPA+personal+trainer+professional+standards) (Reference, Beginner, Search link). UK register of exercise professionals; not opened.
- [REPs Ireland register of exercise professionals (search)](https://www.google.com/search?q=REPs+Ireland+register+of+exercise+professionals+code+of+ethics) (Reference, Beginner, Search link). Irish register and code of ethics; not opened.

### Pass condition
You can place three scenarios in the right scope zone, compute moderate-equivalent minutes correctly, and rate a claim.

## Module 2: Screening, safety and the first consultation

Prerequisites: Module 1. Time: 3 h.

### Core idea
Screening finds the few clients who need a doctor first, without blocking everyone else. The 2015 ACSM algorithm uses exercise status, disease or symptoms and intended intensity, and the first consultation turns this into a safe starting point.

### Tutorial

1. Apply the algorithm to a client.
2. Define regular exercise, intensity bands and symptoms.
3. Compare the algorithm with older tools and name the limits of the evidence.
4. Run a consultation with baseline measures and a stop-and-refer rule.

Worked example: Client C runs four times a week and has felt chest pain twice this month: symptoms, so clearance before continuing.

### Challenges

**C1 (warm-up).** State what counts as 'regular exercise' in the algorithm.
- Answer key: Planned, structured activity of at least 30 minutes at moderate intensity on at least 3 days a week for at least 3 months.
- Self-check: Check against the definitions in lesson 2.

**C2 (core).** Run four invented clients through the flow chart: (a) inactive, no disease, no symptoms; (b) inactive, type 2 diabetes, no symptoms; (c) exercising, no disease, dizziness; (d) exercising, diabetes, no symptoms, wants to start sprinting.
- Answer key: (a) begin light to moderate, no clearance; (b) clearance before any exercise; (c) clearance before continuing; (d) clearance before vigorous.
- Self-check: Compare with the decision rules in lesson 2.

**C3 (stretch).** Write a half-page consultation script with an opening, five screening questions, one baseline measure and a closing action. Include a stop-and-refer rule.
- Answer key: Script includes all five parts; the rule lists chest or arm pain, breathlessness, dizziness or palpitations.
- Self-check: Check that the rule matches the symptoms in the algorithm.

### Common mistakes

- Skipping screening because the client looks fit. Ask everyone.
- Adding conditions to the disease list. If unsure, ask for clearance.
- Treating fewer referrals as proof of a safer tool. It needs validation.

### Review

R1. Name the three scope zones. (Module 1) Answer: Coach, adapt and check, refer out.
R2. What is the 2-to-1 minute trade and where does it come from? (Module 1) Answer: One vigorous minute counts for about two moderate ones; our inference from the 75 versus 150 ranges, not a WHO rule.

### Resources

- [ACSM preparticipation screening algorithm applied to US adults](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7059860/) (Scholarly, Intermediate, Verified). Read abstract, introduction and methods; contains the algorithm figure and definitions.
- [Motivational interviewing: systematic review and meta-analysis](https://europepmc.org/articles/PMC1463134) (Scholarly, Intermediate, Verified). Free full text on Europe PMC; read abstract and excerpts.
- [PAR-Q+ questionnaire (search)](https://www.google.com/search?q=PAR-Q%2B+physical+activity+readiness+questionnaire+official) (Reference, Beginner, Search link). Find the official form; not opened.

### Pass condition
You correctly decide four screening cases and have a consultation script with a stop-and-refer rule.

## Module 3: Motivation, goals and coaching conversations

Prerequisites: Modules 1-2. Time: 3 h.

### Core idea
Programmes fail when clients stop. Support for autonomy, competence and relatedness is linked with lasting change, motivational interviewing beat advice in 72 trials, and clear goals plus tracking keep a plan alive.

### Tutorial

1. Rewrite controlling instructions as choices.
2. Practise open questions, affirmations, reflections and summaries.
3. Turn an outcome goal into performance and process goals with an if-then plan.
4. Track a few markers and respond to setbacks.

Worked example: A ramp from 50 to 150 moderate minutes adds 12.5 minutes a week: 50, 63, 75, 88, 100, 113, 125, 138, 150.

### Challenges

**C1 (warm-up).** Rewrite 'You must train three days a week' as an autonomy-supportive question.
- Answer key: A question that invites the client to design the week, for example 'What would a realistic week look like for you?'
- Self-check: It should offer a choice and keep any safety limit.

**C2 (core).** Take a client goal of 'lose 5 kg' and write an outcome, a performance and two process goals with one if-then plan.
- Answer key: Outcome and performance and two weekly actions; the if-then plan names an obstacle and a response.
- Self-check: Each process goal says what, where and when and can be ticked off.

**C3 (stretch).** Role-play a 5-minute conversation with a partner and count open questions, reflections and advice statements.
- Answer key: At least three open questions and two reflections; advice is offered only after asking permission.
- Self-check: Count from a recording or your partner's tally.

### Common mistakes

- Setting only outcome goals. Add performance and process goals.
- Giving advice before listening. Ask first.
- Ending coaching when a client misses a week. Restart smaller.

### Review

R1. How many moderate-equivalent minutes are 40 vigorous plus 60 moderate? (Module 1) Answer: 140 (60 + 2 x 40).
R2. What advice does the algorithm give for an inactive client with diabetes and no symptoms? (Module 2) Answer: Medical clearance before exercise of any intensity.

### Resources

- [WHO 2020 guidelines on physical activity and sedentary behaviour](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/54/24/1451) (Scholarly, Intermediate, Verified). Read in full (body text). The activity targets and special-population advice.
- [Motivational interviewing: systematic review and meta-analysis](https://europepmc.org/articles/PMC1463134) (Scholarly, Intermediate, Verified). Free full text on Europe PMC; read abstract and excerpts.
- [Self-determination theory applied to health contexts: meta-analysis](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2012_NgNtoumanis_PS.pdf) (Scholarly, Intermediate, Verified). Read abstract and excerpts only.

### Pass condition
You have a three-level goal set, a role-play tally and a tracker for one client.

## Module 4: How training works: load, volume and effort

Prerequisites: Modules 1-3. Time: 3 h.

### Core idea
A resistance programme is built from load, volume, effort, frequency and duration. Large meta-analyses show how load matches the goal, that more sets usually give more growth, and that muscle growth rises nearer failure while strength does not much.

### Tutorial

1. Match load to strength, power or jump goals.
2. Estimate the effect of adding weekly sets with its limits.
3. Use reps in reserve to set effort.
4. Say which findings come from abstracts only.

Worked example: Adding four weekly sets: 4 x 0.37% = 1.48 percentage points more growth on average, per the muscle-size meta-regression. A group-level estimate.

### Challenges

**C1 (warm-up).** Name the five levers and give a starting value for a beginner for each.
- Answer key: Volume, intensity of load, intensity of effort, frequency, duration, each with a value such as 2 to 3 sets, moderate load, 2 to 4 RIR, 2 to 3 sessions, 8 weeks.
- Self-check: Compare with the table in lesson 1.

**C2 (core).** A client has 8 hard sets a week for quads. You add 3 sets. Estimate the extra growth using the 0.37% figure and write one caution.
- Answer key: 3 x 0.37% = 1.11 percentage points; caution: group average, from an abstract, no ceiling stated.
- Self-check: Recompute 3 x 0.37 = 1.11.

**C3 (stretch).** Choose a load and RIR for (a) a jump goal, (b) a strength goal, (c) a power goal and justify each with the evidence.
- Answer key: (a) light, around 30% 1RM; (b) moderate to heavy, near or above 70%; (c) 40 to 70%, with intent; RIR as safe.
- Self-check: Compare with the load table in lesson 2.

### Common mistakes

- Quoting averages as promises.
- Training beginners to failure on free-weight compounds.
- Assuming more exercises means more benefit. Sets on key lifts matter.

### Review

R1. What RPE did the algorithm give for moderate intensity? (Module 2) Answer: 12 to 13.
R2. Which goal level is fully in the client's control? (Module 3) Answer: Process goals.

### Resources

- [Dose-response modelling of resistance exercise](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11239729/) (Scholarly, Advanced, Verified). Read in full. Load, sets, duration.
- [Weekly resistance training volume and muscle mass](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27433992) (Scholarly, Advanced, Verified). Abstract only; full text not freely available.
- [Proximity to failure, strength and hypertrophy meta-regressions](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-024-02069-2) (Scholarly, Advanced, Verified). Abstract only; full text paywalled.

### Pass condition
You can justify a load and RIR for three goals and estimate a set change with its cautions.

## Module 5: Writing and progressing a programme

Prerequisites: Modules 1-4. Time: 4 h.

### Core idea
You turn the levers into sessions and an 8-week plan, count weekly sets, progress safely with double progression, and combine strength work with aerobic activity to reach the WHO targets.

### Tutorial

1. Pick exercises by movement pattern and order them.
2. Write the plan and count weekly sets per group.
3. Apply double progression and respond to plateaus.
4. Add aerobic activity to reach 150 moderate-equivalent minutes.

Worked example: Day A has 3 + 2 + 3 + 3 + 2 = 13 sets; Day A + Day B + Day A is 39 sets a week.

### Challenges

**C1 (warm-up).** Choose five exercises that cover squat, hinge, push, pull and trunk patterns with a basic gym.
- Answer key: One exercise per pattern, such as goblet squat, Romanian deadlift, press, row, plank.
- Self-check: All five patterns are present.

**C2 (core).** Write Day A and Day B for a client with dumbbells only. Count hard sets per day and per week for two days.
- Answer key: Each day 11 to 15 sets with all patterns; weekly total is the sum of both days.
- Self-check: Add up your own sets; they must match your table.

**C3 (stretch).** Write 8 weeks of progression for the row, using double progression from 8 to 12 reps, with a plateau response.
- Answer key: Reps rise week by week to 12, then load increases and reps reset; a plateau rule such as check sleep then lighter week.
- Self-check: Each week's numbers follow the rule.

### Common mistakes

- Adding load before the top of the rep range.
- Ignoring pain. Stop and refer.
- Counting only strength days and forgetting aerobic minutes.

### Review

R1. How close to failure do beginners start? (Module 4) Answer: Well short: about 3 to 4 reps in reserve in the first weeks (our default).
R2. How do you handle a missed week? (Module 3) Answer: Ask without judgement, reflect, and restart with a smaller version of the plan.

### Resources

- [WHO 2020 guidelines on physical activity and sedentary behaviour](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/54/24/1451) (Scholarly, Intermediate, Verified). Read in full (body text). The activity targets and special-population advice.
- [Dose-response modelling of resistance exercise](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11239729/) (Scholarly, Advanced, Verified). Read in full. Load, sets, duration.
- [UK Chief Medical Officers' physical activity guidelines](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/physical-activity-guidelines-uk-chief-medical-officers-report) (Reference, Beginner, Verified). Page opened; only the update history was read. Check the current adult wording yourself.
- [Personal training technique videos (search)](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=goblet+squat+technique+coach) (Video, Beginner, Search link). No specific video was verified; choose from a qualified coach and check it matches your client.

### Pass condition
You have an 8-week plan with counted sets, a progression rule and weekly aerobic minutes.

## Module 6: Nutrition, recovery and special populations

Prerequisites: Modules 1-5. Time: 3 h.

### Core idea
Clients ask about food and about their special circumstances. This module gives the protein evidence with its limits, the scope boundary for nutrition advice, and the WHO guidance for older adults, pregnancy, long-term conditions and disability.

### Tutorial

1. Compute a daily protein target and range from body weight.
2. Explain which subgroups benefited and which did not.
3. Decide whether a nutrition question is in scope.
4. Adapt sessions for an older adult or other special group.

Worked example: A 70 kg client: 1.6 x 70 = 112 g, with a plausible range of 72 to 154 g a day.

### Challenges

**C1 (warm-up).** Calculate protein at 0.8, 1.6 and 2.2 g/kg for a 65 kg client.
- Answer key: 52 g, 104 g, 143 g.
- Self-check: 65 x 0.8 = 52; 65 x 1.6 = 104; 65 x 2.2 = 143.

**C2 (core).** Write a script declining a diet request from a client with a kidney condition while still helping with training.
- Answer key: Explains you cannot advise on medical diets, suggests their doctor or dietitian, and offers to train within their limits.
- Self-check: Check it neither prescribes nor dismisses.

**C3 (stretch).** Adapt Day A for a 72-year-old inactive client with no disease or symptoms. State sessions per week and the WHO source.
- Answer key: Three sessions a week with balance, strength and walking; cites 3 or more days of multicomponent activity.
- Self-check: Check against lesson 4.

### Common mistakes

- Prescribing diets for medical conditions.
- Telling dieting clients the muscle-gain numbers apply to them.
- Ignoring clearance for clients with conditions.

### Review

R1. What does the algorithm say about an inactive client with diabetes? (Module 2) Answer: Clearance before any exercise.
R2. What loads favour jumping? (Module 4) Answer: Light loads, around 30% of 1RM.

### Resources

- [WHO 2020 guidelines on physical activity and sedentary behaviour](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/54/24/1451) (Scholarly, Intermediate, Verified). Read in full (body text). The activity targets and special-population advice.
- [Protein supplementation and resistance training gains: meta-analysis](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/52/6/376) (Scholarly, Intermediate, Verified). Read in full. Break point 1.62 g/kg/day.
- [CIMSPA professional standards (search)](https://www.google.com/search?q=CIMSPA+personal+trainer+professional+standards) (Reference, Beginner, Search link). UK register of exercise professionals; not opened.
- [REPs Ireland register of exercise professionals (search)](https://www.google.com/search?q=REPs+Ireland+register+of+exercise+professionals+code+of+ethics) (Reference, Beginner, Search link). Irish register and code of ethics; not opened.

### Pass condition
You can compute a protein target, decline an out-of-scope request correctly and adapt a session for a special group.

## Capstone: Build and defend an 8-week coaching file for a case-study client

Build and defend an 8-week coaching file for a case-study client: screening, goals, programme, weekly activity, scope note and review.

**Deliverable:** Complete the screening record.; Write goals, the 8-week plan and the weekly activity plan.; Score against the rubric and write the review..

**Rubric**

- Screening and goals: Show the algorithm decision with reasons and three goal levels with an if-then plan. Passing result: Decision is correct; goals include outcome, performance and process.
- Programme and activity: Show the 8-week plan with sets, reps, RIR and progression plus a weekly activity plan that reaches the WHO range. Passing result: Sets counted; RIR stated; minutes sum to at least 150 by week 9.
- Scope and review: Show a scope and referral note and a week-4 review. Passing result: Out-of-scope items named; two checks and one change listed.

**Pass condition:** Rubric score of at least 8 out of 10 with no zeros and the review written.
