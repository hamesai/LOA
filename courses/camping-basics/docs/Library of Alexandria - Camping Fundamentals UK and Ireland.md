## Module 1: Where you may camp, and leaving no trace

Prerequisites: None. Time: 3 h.

### Core idea
In Ireland all land is owned and access depends on the owner's goodwill; in Scotland and on parts of Dartmoor camping is allowed with conditions; elsewhere in the UK you generally need permission or a campsite. Leave No Trace turns that into habits for fire, toilet, water and waste.

### Tutorial

1. Find out who owns and manages the ground and which rule applies.
2. Choose a serviced campsite for a first trip and book a backup.
3. Plan fire, toilet, water and rubbish before you leave.

Worked example: Three spots (a Mayo farm field, a permitted Dartmoor pitch, a Highland lay-by): the field needs the farmer's permission, the Dartmoor pitch needs a map check, and the lay-by is ruled out for overnight sleeping in a car.

### Challenges

**C1 (warm-up).** Pick a place you might camp. Write the owner or manager, and the rule that applies.
- Answer key: A named owner or manager, the camping rule in one sentence, and the source of the rule.
- Self-check: Can a stranger confirm the rule from your source in under two minutes?

**C2 (core).** Write one action for each of the seven Leave No Trace principles for your first trip.
- Answer key: Seven actions, one per principle, each specific (for example: bag all food waste).
- Self-check: Count seven distinct, concrete actions.

**C3 (stretch).** Write a toilet and fire plan for a two-night trip somewhere with no facilities.
- Answer key: A trowel and bags, a distance from water of at least 30 m, a burial depth of at least 15 cm or a carry-out plan, and a stove-only fire rule.
- Self-check: Check all four items are present and match the sources.

### Common mistakes

- Assuming no sign means permission → ask the owner or manager.
- Lighting a fire in dry weather → use a stove.

### Resources

- [The Countryside Code (England and Wales)](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-countryside-code/the-countryside-code-advice-for-countryside-visitors) (Reference, Beginner, Verified). Respect, protect, enjoy.

- [Camping in Ireland](https://www.leavenotraceireland.org/love-this-place-leave-no-trace/camping-ireland) (Reference, Beginner, Verified). Permission, national parks, Coillte.

### Pass condition

## Module 2: Shelter: choosing, pitching, staying dry

Prerequisites: Module 1. Time: 3 h.

### Core idea
A tent is sized by sleeping pads, not by comfort. Where you pitch matters as much as what you pitch: wind, water and trees decide whether you stay dry. Condensation is warm humid air meeting a cold fly.

### Tutorial

1. Choose a tent with one size more space than the number of sleepers.
2. Pick higher, drier, sheltered ground away from damaged trees.
3. Pitch in order, tension the fly evenly, add guylines in daylight.

Worked example: Two adults and a dog need about 40 to 60 square feet (3.7 to 5.6 m²), so a three-person three-season tent with a full fly.

### Challenges

**C1 (warm-up).** Look at a tent's specification and write its person rating, floor area, season rating and fly type.
- Answer key: Four values read from the specification.
- Self-check: Compare with the REI size table.

**C2 (core).** Pitch your tent at home twice. Time it and note what slowed you.
- Answer key: Second pitch quicker than the first, fly taut with seams aligned.
- Self-check: Check that fly seams line up with the tent body and poles.

**C3 (stretch).** Choose between two imaginary pitches (low by a stream, or a rise with trees). Write three reasons.
- Answer key: The rise: drier, windbreak, less condensation.
- Self-check: Each reason must name a hazard from the lesson.

### Common mistakes

- Pitching in a hollow → move to higher, drier ground.
- Skipping fly re-tension after rain → re-check the fly after it gets wet.

### Review

R1. What should you check before drinking tap water at a campsite? Answer: Whether it is treated; treat it if not.

### Resources

- [Camping for Beginners](https://www.rei.com/learn/expert-advice/camping-for-beginners.html) (Reference, Beginner, Verified). Gear, clothing, meals, where to camp.
- [How to Choose a Camping Tent](https://www.rei.com/learn/expert-advice/family-base-camping-tent.html) (Reference, Beginner, Verified). Capacity, seasons, shapes.
- [How to Set Up a Tent](https://www.rei.com/learn/expert-advice/tent-set-up.html) (Reference, Beginner, Verified). Site, pitching, guylines.
- [How to Prevent Condensation in a Tent](https://www.rei.com/learn/expert-advice/how-to-prevent-condensation-in-a-tent.html) (Reference, Beginner, Verified). Three strategies.
- [How to choose a camping tent (video search)](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=how+to+choose+a+camping+tent+REI) (Video, Beginner, Search link). Video titles not verified. REI publishes tent videos.

### Pass condition

## Module 3: The sleep system and staying warm

Prerequisites: Modules 1 and 2. Time: 3 h.

### Core idea
Warmth is a system: bag, pad and clothing. Comfort and lower-limit ratings are estimates for cold and warm sleepers, bag ratings assume a pad of R 4, and R-values add when pads are stacked. Wind, height, cold ground and wet all take heat from you.

### Tutorial

1. Read both rating numbers on a bag.
2. Match pad R-value to the forecast and add pads if needed.
3. Prepare for bed in dry layers with a warm drink, and know the hypothermia signs.

Worked example: An air pad R 2.5 plus a foam pad R 2.0 gives R 4.5, which meets the R 4 assumed by bag tests.

### Challenges

**C1 (warm-up).** Find the comfort and lower-limit ratings of one bag, or note that it has only one number.
- Answer key: Two numbers, or a note that the single number is the brand's estimate.
- Self-check: Which rating is lower?

**C2 (core).** A hill pitch is 450 m above the valley where the forecast low is 8 °C. Estimate the pitch low.
- Answer key: 450 ÷ 300 × 2 = 3 °C cooler, so about 5 °C before wind chill.
- Self-check: Check you divided by 300 and multiplied by 2.

**C3 (stretch).** Write a night routine for a 2 °C night, and the hypothermia signs and response.
- Answer key: Dry layers, snack and warm drink, hat, hood cinched; signs: shivering, slurred speech, confusion; response: shelter, wrap, call 999.
- Self-check: Check no step involves alcohol, rubbing limbs or a hot bath.

### Common mistakes

- Reading the number in a bag's name as its rating → read the tested rating.
- Overdressing inside the bag → use dry base layers and drape a jacket on top.

### Review

R1. Where should you pitch a tent to reduce condensation? Answer: On higher, drier ground, preferably under trees.
R2. What is the minimum distance REI advises from lakes and streams? Answer: 200 feet, about 61 m.

### Resources

- [Understanding Sleeping Bag Temperature Ratings](https://www.rei.com/learn/expert-advice/understanding-sleeping-bag-temperature-ratings.html) (Reference, Beginner, Verified). Comfort vs lower limit.
- [Sleeping Pads: How to Choose](https://www.rei.com/learn/expert-advice/sleeping-pads.html) (Reference, Beginner, Verified). R-values and types (read in part).
- [How to Sleep Well While Camping](https://www.rei.com/learn/expert-advice/camp-sleeping-tips.html) (Reference, Beginner, Verified). Staying warm at night.
- [Wind chill factor](https://weather.metoffice.gov.uk/learn-about/weather/types-of-weather/wind/wind-chill-factor) (Reference, Beginner, Verified). Feels-like temperature.
- [Hypothermia](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/hypothermia/) (Reference, Beginner, Verified). Symptoms and what to do.

### Pass condition
Ratings read (C1), temperature estimate correct (C2), and a night routine with hypothermia plan (C3).

## Module 4: The camp kitchen: stoves, water, food

Prerequisites: Modules 1 to 3. Time: 3 h.

### Core idea
A camp kitchen has three serious risks: fire, carbon monoxide and bad water or food. Cook outside, treat water of unknown quality, keep food cold and cooked through.

### Tutorial

1. Inspect and test the stove away from the tent and check the flame colour.
2. Treat water by boiling, or filter then disinfect.
3. Plan food to stay below 5 °C and cook it right through.

Worked example: A mostly orange flame is a warning of poor combustion and carbon monoxide: stop and check before using the stove.

### Challenges

**C1 (warm-up).** Test a stove outdoors. Write the flame colour and the checks you made.
- Answer key: Blue base with a slight orange tip, and the checks listed in the lesson.
- Self-check: Did you check for loose parts, O-rings, a closed valve and a leak test?

**C2 (core).** Write a water plan: source, treatment and backup.
- Answer key: Treat water of unknown origin by boiling for a minute; filter then disinfect as a backup.
- Self-check: Is every source treated or confirmed treated?

**C3 (stretch).** Write a two-night menu with raw and ready-to-eat foods separated, a chilling plan and cooking checks.
- Answer key: Perishables eaten first, a plan to stay below 5 °C, and steaming hot, no pink, clear juices.
- Self-check: Check each rule from the food table appears.

### Common mistakes

- Bringing a stove or barbecue into the tent → cook outside.
- Trusting clear water → treat it.

### Review

R1. What does the Met Office say is the average cooling per 300 m of climb? Answer: About 2 °C, but it varies.
R2. What is the rule for guylines? Answer: At least on the windward side, over the guy-out points, rigged before dark.

### Resources

- [Being a cool camper](https://www.outdooraccess-scotland.scot/practical-guide-all/camping) (Reference, Beginner, Verified). Scotland's camping allowance, fire, toilet and parking advice.
- [Camping for Beginners](https://www.rei.com/learn/expert-advice/camping-for-beginners.html) (Reference, Beginner, Verified). Gear, clothing, meals, where to camp.
- [Camping stove safety](https://www.thebmc.co.uk/en/camping-stove-safety-how-to-avoid-carbon-monoxide-and-fire-risks) (Reference, Beginner, Verified). Checks, flame colour, carbon monoxide.
- [Carbon monoxide dangers if camping](https://www.nidirect.gov.uk/news/carbon-monoxide-dangers-if-camping-caravanning-or-boating) (Reference, Beginner, Verified). Barbecues, symptoms, action.
- [Water treatment when hiking, camping or travelling](https://www.cdc.gov/drinking-water/prevention/water-treatment-hiking-camping-traveling.html) (Reference, Beginner, Verified). Boil, filter, disinfect.
- [Camping food safety](https://food.blog.gov.uk/2020/07/20/camping-food-safety) (Reference, Beginner, Verified). Chill, cook, clean.

### Pass condition
Stove checked (C1), water plan (C2), and menu (C3) complete.

## Module 5: Navigation, timing and weather limits

Prerequisites: Modules 1 to 4. Time: 3 h.

### Core idea
A paper map and a compass work when a phone fails. Scale tells you distance, a grid reference tells you where you are, a bearing tells you which way to go, and Naismith's rule tells you how long it will take.

### Tutorial

1. Measure a route with string and the scale.
2. Give a six-figure grid reference.
3. Set a bearing and estimate walking time.

Worked example: 10 km with 450 m of climbing: 10 ÷ 5 = 2 h plus 450 ÷ 600 = 0.75 h, a total of 2 h 45.

### Challenges

**C1 (warm-up).** On a 1:25,000 map, a route measures 10 cm with string. How far is it?
- Answer key: 10 ÷ 4 = 2.5 km.
- Self-check: Check you divided by 4.

**C2 (core).** Give the six-figure grid reference for a point 7 tenths across and 2 up in square 51 84.
- Answer key: 517 842.
- Self-check: Eastings 51 then 7, northings 84 then 2.

**C3 (stretch).** Plan a 12 km walk with 600 m of climbing. Give the Naismith time and set a turn-back time to be home before dark at 18:30.
- Answer key: 12 ÷ 5 = 2.4 h; 600 ÷ 600 = 1 h; total 3.4 h = 3 h 24. Add rests. Start by about 14:00 to finish by 17:30 with rests (illustrative).
- Self-check: Check the arithmetic and that a margin exists before dark.

### Common mistakes

- Reading northing first → along the corridor, then up the stairs.
- Moving the bezel after setting → set it once, then turn your body.

### Review

R1. What temperature is hypothermia? Answer: Body temperature below 35 °C.
R2. Why not rub a hypothermic person's limbs? Answer: The NHS says it will not help and could make things worse.

### Resources

- [Map reading made easy](https://www.ordnancesurvey.co.uk/documents/resources/map-reading-made-easy.pdf) (Reference, Beginner, Verified). Scale, grid references.
- [Beginners guide to using a compass](https://getoutside.ordnancesurvey.co.uk/guides/beginners-guide-to-using-a-compass/) (Reference, Beginner, Verified). Four steps to a bearing.
- [Why we're adjusting Naismith's Rule](https://www.ordnancesurvey.co.uk/blog/why-were-adjusting-naismiths-rule) (Reference, Intermediate, Verified). The rule and its limits.
- [Mountain safety](https://weather.metoffice.gov.uk/guides/mountain/video) (Reference, Beginner, Verified). AdventureSmart, wind, cloud.

## Module 6: Safety, illness and calling for help

Prerequisites: Modules 1 to 5. Time: 3 h.

### Core idea
Safety is made of small habits: ask the three questions, leave a trip card, turn back early, recognise the signs of cold, gas and dirty water, and know how to call for help.

### Tutorial

1. Complete a trip card and send it to someone.
2. List three hazards at a pitch with controls.
3. Write a call card and rehearse it.

Worked example: Three campers with headaches after a night with a stove in the tent: suspect carbon monoxide, get outside, ventilate, seek help.

### Challenges

**C1 (warm-up).** Write a trip card for your first trip and send it to a friend.
- Answer key: Place, route, people, vehicle, back-by time and action time.
- Self-check: Does the friend know exactly what to do if you do not call?

**C2 (core).** Sketch a pitch you know and mark three hazards with a control for each.
- Answer key: Three hazards from the lesson table, each with a control.
- Self-check: Are the controls actions you can take?

**C3 (stretch).** Fill in a call card for an imagined incident and say it aloud in under a minute.
- Answer key: Location with grid reference, casualty details, injury, party size, phone number; Police then Mountain Rescue.
- Self-check: Time yourself.

### Common mistakes

- Walking away from a casualty to find help → stay and call 999.
- Treating group illness as coincidence → suspect a shared cause.

### Review

R1. What is the NHS advice for hypothermia while waiting for help? Answer: Shelter, remove wet clothing, wrap, warm sweet drink if alert, keep them awake.
R2. What does a mostly orange stove flame mean? Answer: Poor oxygen supply or a malfunction, producing more carbon monoxide.

## Capstone: Plan, pack and review a first overnight camp

Plan, pack and (if possible) run a one-night camp on a serviced site. The plan must show access and impact, shelter and warmth, kitchen and water, navigation, and safety.

**Deliverable:** Write the plan with citations to the rules you apply.; Pack a kit from a checklist and test the stove and tent.; Run the trip and write a one-page review..

**Rubric**

- Access and impact: State the site, whose land it is, the rule and the Leave No Trace actions. Passing result: Site, owner, rule and seven actions are all present and sourced.
- Shelter and warmth: Show tent size, pitch reasoning, and bag and pad matched to the forecast. Passing result: Reasoned size, pitch and R-value, with the forecast at the pitch height.
- Kitchen, water, navigation and safety: Include stove checks, water plan, food temperature plan, a six-figure reference, a Naismith estimate, a trip card and a call card. Passing result: All seven items are present and correct.

**Pass condition:** All three rubric rows meet the passing result and the review is written.
