## Sources read for this course (updated 15/10/2026)

"Read in part" means the passages relevant to the lesson were read through a fetched copy of the page or file. It does not mean every page was read.

**Primary texts by the lecturers**
- Harry Markowitz, Portfolio Selection, Journal of Finance 7(1), March 1952. Read through a fetched PDF copy: the two-stage framing, the rejection of the maximise-expected-return rule, the expected return and variance rule, and the diversification passages. The geometric figures and the appendix were not worked through.
- Louis Bachelier, Théorie de la spéculation (1900, in French, Numdam). Read the introduction and sampled later sections. The mathematics was not worked through. Wikipedia is cited once, for the later description of the model as a random walk.
- Benjamin Graham, The Intelligent Investor, fourth revised edition (1973), with Jason Zweig's commentary, from an Internet Archive copy. Read the Chapter 1 definition of investment against speculation and the Chapter 20 margin-of-safety passages, plus Zweig's notes. The rest of the book was not read.
- Irving Fisher, The Rate of Interest (1907), Internet Archive. Read chapter 1 and passages from chapters 6 and 7. His 1930 The Theory of Interest is access-restricted there and was not read; only its title is used, from the catalogue record.
- John C. Bogle, speeches of 20/07/1997 (costs matter), 20/05/2000 and 09/07/2016 (Vanguard's founding and the first index fund), from the Bogle Financial Markets Research Center. Read in part.
- Federal Reserve History, Stock Market Crash of 1929, for Fisher's statement as reported in the New York Times on 16/10/1929, and the Dow's rise and fall.

**Official UK pages (read in part)**
- MoneyHelper: A beginner's guide to investing (12/10/2026); How to spot an investment scam (12/10/2026); Types of investments (13/10/2026).
- FCA InvestSmart: Diversification (16/05/2025); Risk and returns (19/01/2026). FCA: Protect yourself from scams (19/01/2026); press release of 29/01/2018.
- GOV.UK: Individual Savings Accounts (£20,000 for 2026 to 2027); Capital Gains Tax rates and allowances (annual exempt amount £3,000 for 2026 to 2027, from the guidance table).

**Data**
- S&P Dow Jones Indices, SPIVA page, data as of 30/06/2026. Read the US summary table only. The full scorecard was not read. US funds only.

**Secondary (Wikipedia, read as excerpts), used for three claims only**
- Bogle: the First Index Investment Trust dated to 1976. Bogle's own 2016 speech says the first index mutual fund was formed in 1975, so the sources differ by a year.
- Fisher: the crash and Depression cost him much of his wealth and reputation.
- Bachelier and Markowitz: the later labels "random walk" and "efficient frontier".

**Source disagreements found**
- The FCA states its 10% rule of thumb on two bases: 10% of net assets (Risk and returns) and 10% of your portfolio (Diversification). Module 3 shows both.
- Graham's own definition says an "adequate" return. Secondary summaries say "satisfactory", and the course uses "adequate".

**Not verified**
- The rule of 72 is our own arithmetic and a rule of thumb, not a sourced standard.
- Bogle's dollar cost illustration is his; we did not see the growth assumptions behind it.
- Graham's 1965 and 1972 earnings-yield figures are as quoted in the book's notes, not our calculation of market data.
- Birth and death years for Markowitz, Bogle and Graham come from Wikipedia or a news headline, not a primary record. Fisher's and Bachelier's years come from the Internet Archive catalogue.
- No UK fund-level evidence was read. The SPIVA figures are US only.
