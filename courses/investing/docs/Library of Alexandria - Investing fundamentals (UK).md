## Module 1: Saving, investing and the first money decisions

Prerequisites: None. Time: 3 h.

### Core idea
Investing only makes sense once cash is safe. This module separates saving from investing, sets an emergency fund and shows why cash can lose buying power.

### Tutorial

1. Define saving and investing with time horizon and risk.
2. Calculate a three-month and six-month emergency fund.
3. Compare an interest rate with inflation.

Worked example: A £1,500 monthly spend gives a six-month fund of £9,000. Cash at 1% with 3% inflation loses about 2% of buying power a year.

### Challenges

**C1 (warm-up).** Your essential spending is £1,800 a month. Give your three-month and six-month emergency fund targets.
- Answer key: £5,400 and £10,800.
- Self-check: Multiply £1,800 by 3, then by 6.

**C2 (core).** Explain, in three sentences, why an emergency fund comes before investing.
- Answer key: A good answer says the fund covers shocks without forced selling, names the three to six month range, and links it to a time horizon of five years or more.
- Self-check: Check that you mention forced selling and the time horizon.

**C3 (stretch).** £10,000 earns 2% and inflation is 4%. Estimate the change in buying power after one year, using the approximation, and say why it is only an estimate.
- Answer key: About minus 2%, or roughly £9,800 in today's money. It is an approximation, because the exact figure is £10,200 ÷ 1.04 = £9,807.69.
- Self-check: The exact answer is 10,200 divided by 1.04, which is about 9,807.69.

### Common mistakes

- Investing the emergency fund. Fix it by keeping cash reachable within days.
- Judging cash only by its pound value. Fix it by comparing the rate with inflation.

### Resources

- [A beginner's guide to investing](https://www.moneyhelper.org.uk/en/savings/investing/investing-beginners-guide) (Reference, Beginner, Verified). Read as published.
- [The Rate of Interest (1907)](https://archive.org/details/rateofinterestit00fishuoft) (Book, Intermediate, Verified). Chapter 1 and passages from chapters 6 and 7 read.

### Pass condition
The learner writes a three-month and six-month emergency fund target, and states the rate-minus-inflation test.

## Module 2: How returns and compounding work

Prerequisites: Module 1. Time: 3 h.

### Core idea
Returns come from income and price change. Compounding and fees decide how much of them you keep.

### Tutorial

1. List the four sources of return.
2. Compound a sum over several years.
3. Measure the effect of an annual fee.

Worked example: £1,000 at a steady 5% becomes £1,628.89 after 10 years and £2,653.30 after 20. A 1.5% fee over 30 years turns £43,219 into £28,068.

### Challenges

**C1 (warm-up).** A fund pays £25 in dividends and its price falls £60. What is the total return before fees?
- Answer key: A loss of £35.
- Self-check: £25 income minus £60 price fall is −£35.

**C2 (core).** Compound £2,000 at 4% for 15 years. Show the formula.
- Answer key: £2,000 × 1.04^15 = £3,601.9, to the nearest pound £3,602.
- Self-check: Use a calculator for 1.04 to the power of 15, about 1.8009.

**C3 (stretch).** Compare a 0.2% charge with a 1.2% charge on £20,000 invested for 25 years at a gross 6%. Give the difference in pounds and say what you assumed.
- Answer key: Net 5.8% gives about £81,879; net 4.8% gives about £64,575. Difference about £17,304. Assumes gross 6% every year, no tax.
- Self-check: Compute 20,000 × 1.058^25 and 20,000 × 1.048^25, then subtract. The results are about £81,879 and £64,575.

### Common mistakes

- Quoting the headline return and ignoring fees. Fix it by using the net return.
- Treating a steady-return example as a forecast. Fix it by labelling it illustrative.

### Review

R1. Name the four sources of return. Answer: Dividends, rent, interest and capital gains or losses.

### Resources

- [A beginner's guide to investing](https://www.moneyhelper.org.uk/en/savings/investing/investing-beginners-guide) (Reference, Beginner, Verified). Read as published.
- [Who's in Charge Here: Asset Allocation or Cost?](https://boglecenter.net/wp-content/uploads/AIMR-7-20-97.pdf) (Reference, Intermediate, Verified). Read in part; his cost illustration.
- [The Rate of Interest (1907)](https://archive.org/details/rateofinterestit00fishuoft) (Book, Intermediate, Verified). Chapter 1 and passages from chapters 6 and 7 read.

### Pass condition
The learner computes a compound value and a fee difference in pounds, and states every assumption.

## Module 3: Risk, volatility and random walks

Prerequisites: Modules 1 and 2. Time: 3 h.

### Core idea
Risk is the chance of loss and the swings along the way. Recovery from a fall needs a bigger rise, and short-term prices are hard to predict.

### Tutorial

1. Distinguish risk tolerance from risk capacity.
2. Apply the FCA 10% rule of thumb.
3. Calculate the gain needed to recover from a fall.

Worked example: A 30% fall needs a 42.9% rise to recover: £10,000 falls to £7,000, and £3,000 ÷ £7,000 is 42.9%.

### Challenges

**C1 (warm-up).** Your net assets are £30,000, with no debts. What is the 10% rule of thumb limit for high-risk investments?
- Answer key: £3,000.
- Self-check: Multiply £30,000 by 10%.

**C2 (core).** A portfolio falls 20%, then rises 20%. Where does it end, and why?
- Answer key: It ends at 96% of the start, so it does not recover. 0.8 × 1.2 = 0.96.
- Self-check: Multiply the two factors.

**C3 (stretch).** Write one paragraph separating your risk tolerance from your risk capacity, using a realistic example.
- Answer key: Tolerance is emotional and capacity is financial. A good answer shows different answers and the one that governs.
- Self-check: Check the paragraph names both and gives a case where they differ.

### Common mistakes

- Confusing a fall and a rise of the same size. Fix it with the recovery table.
- Treating the 10% rule as a legal limit. Fix it by calling it a rule of thumb.

### Review

R1. At a steady 5% a year, roughly how long does money take to double? Answer: About 14 years by the rule of 72; the exact figure is about 14.2 years.

### Resources

- [Risk and returns](https://www.fca.org.uk/investsmart/risk-returns) (Reference, Beginner, Verified). Read in part; the 10% rule.
- [Théorie de la spéculation](https://www.numdam.org/item/ASENS_1900_3_17__21_0) (Scholarly, Advanced, Verified). Introduction and sampled sections read; mathematics not worked through.

### Pass condition
The learner computes a recovery gain and applies the 10% rule to a stated net-asset figure.

## Module 4: Diversification and building a portfolio

Prerequisites: Modules 1 to 3. Time: 3 h.

### Core idea
Diversification spreads money across holdings that do not rely on the same things. Markowitz gave the idea a mathematical form, and a simple plan can be explained in one paragraph.

### Tutorial

1. Describe three levels of diversification.
2. Explain the efficient frontier in plain terms.
3. Write a portfolio description you can review.

Worked example: A £5,000 pot split 60/30/10 across shares, bonds and cash is £3,000, £1,500 and £500. It is an example to study, not advice.

### Challenges

**C1 (warm-up).** Apply a 60/30/10 split to £8,000. Give each slice.
- Answer key: £4,800, £2,400 and £800.
- Self-check: Multiply 8,000 by 0.6, 0.3 and 0.1.

**C2 (core).** Explain, without jargon, why two holdings that move apart can lower portfolio risk.
- Answer key: They do not fall together, so a loss in one may be offset by the other. Diversification cannot remove a market-wide fall.
- Self-check: Check the answer mentions both the offset and the limit.

**C3 (stretch).** Write a one-paragraph portfolio description with a review date and two triggers for change.
- Answer key: A complete paragraph names the split, the reason, the review date and the triggers.
- Self-check: All four elements must appear.

### Common mistakes

- Calling diversification a guarantee. Fix it with the limit stated by the FCA.
- Holding many funds that all track the same thing. Fix it by checking what each fund holds.

### Review

R1. What is the 10% rule of thumb? Answer: Limit high-risk investments to about 10% of net assets.

### Resources

- [Diversification](https://www.fca.org.uk/investsmart/diversification) (Reference, Beginner, Verified). Read in part.
- [Portfolio Selection](http://www.efalken.com/LowVolClassics/markowitz_JF1952.pdf) (Scholarly, Advanced, Verified). Free PDF copy; argument and diversification passages read.

### Pass condition
The learner describes diversification, its limits, and a written portfolio with a review date.

## Module 5: Costs, funds and tax wrappers

Prerequisites: Modules 1 to 4. Time: 3 h.

### Core idea
The return you keep is after costs and tax. This module covers funds, the SPIVA evidence on active funds with its limits, and the ISA and Capital Gains Tax allowances.

### Tutorial

1. Name the main types of pooled fund.
2. Read the SPIVA table and state its limits.
3. Apply the ISA and Capital Gains Tax allowances.

Worked example: A £8,000 gain outside an ISA, with a £3,000 exempt amount, leaves £5,000 before losses and reliefs. The ISA limit for 2026 to 2027 is £20,000.

### Challenges

**C1 (warm-up).** Name two ways to hold a pooled fund in the UK.
- Answer key: Any two of unit trust, OEIC, investment trust, ISA or workplace pension.
- Self-check: Check against the module table.

**C2 (core).** The SPIVA figure for 10 years is 83.33%. What does it mean, and what does it not show?
- Answer key: It means that 83.33% of active US large-cap funds underperformed the S&P 500 over 10 years. It does not show UK funds or any single fund's future.
- Self-check: The answer must give both the meaning and a limit.

**C3 (stretch).** Gain £12,000 outside an ISA, with a £3,000 exempt amount. Give the taxable gain before rates, and name two things that could reduce it.
- Answer key: £9,000 before losses and reliefs. Two of: allowable losses, reliefs, costs of disposal.
- Self-check: Subtract the exempt amount, then name reductions.

### Common mistakes

- Using US evidence as UK advice. Fix it by labelling the market.
- Forgetting survivorship. Fix it by counting funds that closed.

### Review

R1. What does a fund's net return mean? Answer: The return after the charges are taken out.

### Resources

- [Individual Savings Accounts (ISAs)](https://www.gov.uk/individual-savings-accounts) (Reference, Beginner, Verified). The 2026 to 2027 limit.
- [Capital Gains Tax allowances](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/capital-gains-tax-rates-and-allowances) (Reference, Beginner, Verified). Table gives £3,000 for 2026 to 2027; search extract read.
- [SPIVA U.S. Scorecard](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/research-insights/spiva/) (Scholarly, Intermediate, Verified). US funds, data as of 30 June 2026; summary table read.
- [Who's in Charge Here: Asset Allocation or Cost?](https://boglecenter.net/wp-content/uploads/AIMR-7-20-97.pdf) (Reference, Intermediate, Verified). Read in part; his cost illustration.
- [Saving a Company, Building a Colossus, Preserving a Culture](https://boglecenter.net/wp-content/uploads/65th-Anniversary-7-9-16.pdf) (Reference, Beginner, Verified). Vanguard founding and the first index fund, in his words.

### Pass condition
The learner reads the SPIVA table with its limits, and applies the ISA and CGT allowances to a worked case.

## Module 6: Discipline, margin of safety and avoiding scams

Prerequisites: Modules 1 to 5. Time: 3 h.

### Core idea
Most beginner losses come from behaviour and fraud. Graham's distinction, the margin of safety and the FCA checks give you a defence.

### Tutorial

1. Apply the investment and speculation test.
2. Explain the margin of safety.
3. Run the FCA Firm Checker and Warning List before paying anyone.

Worked example: An estimate of £100 and a purchase at £75 gives a 25% margin. A value 20% lower, at £80, still leaves room.

### Challenges

**C1 (warm-up).** A stranger offers a guaranteed 15% a month on social media. Name three warning signs.
- Answer key: Unsolicited, guaranteed return, pressure, and a high promised rate. Any three.
- Self-check: Check against the warning signs in the module.

**C2 (core).** Estimate an asset at £120. What purchase price gives a 25% margin of safety?
- Answer key: £90, since 120 × 0.75 = 90.
- Self-check: Multiply your estimate by one minus the margin.

**C3 (stretch).** Write the checks you would make on a firm you found online, in order.
- Answer key: Firm Checker, then Warning List, then a call-back on a number you found yourself, then impartial advice. Order and reasons both matter.
- Self-check: All four steps appear, in order, with reasons.

### Common mistakes

- Trusting a firm because it has a website. Fix it with the FCA Firm Checker.
- Paying a fee to recover earlier losses. Fix it by treating the offer as a scam sign.

### Review

R1. What does an index tracker aim to do? Answer: Copy the performance of a market index; it does not try to beat it.

### Resources

- [How to spot an investment scam](https://www.moneyhelper.org.uk/en/money-troubles/scams/how-to-spot-an-investment-scam) (Reference, Beginner, Verified). Read in part.
- [Protect yourself from scams](https://www.fca.org.uk/consumers/protect-yourself-scams) (Reference, Beginner, Verified). Read in part.
- [The Intelligent Investor (4th revised edition, 1973)](https://archive.org/details/BooksInvesting) (Book, Intermediate, Verified). Chapters 1 and 20 passages read; the rest not read.
- [Stock Market Crash of 1929](https://www.federalreservehistory.org/essays/stock-market-crash-of-1929) (Reference, Beginner, Verified). Fisher's statement, the Dow's rise and Black Monday.

### Pass condition
The learner applies the speculation test, calculates a margin, and writes the scam checks in order.

## Capstone: Your written investment plan

Write a one-page investment plan, checked against the rubric and the course's sources.

**Deliverable:** Write the plan on one page.; Score it against the rubric.; Name what would change it..

**Rubric**

- Plan completeness: All eight sections are written: goal, safety net, risk, portfolio, costs, tax, scam checks and review. Passing result: Each section has a sentence of reasoning.
- Arithmetic: Costs, the emergency fund and the risk limit are calculated, with working shown. Passing result: Every number traces to a formula in the course.
- Honesty: Every assumption is labelled, and the plan says where it stops applying. Passing result: Illustrative and inference labels appear where needed.

**Pass condition:** All rubric rows meet the passing result, and the plan is ready to take to regulated advice if the learner chooses.

**Source disagreements found**
- The FCA states its 10% rule of thumb on two bases: 10% of net assets (Risk and returns) and 10% of your portfolio (Diversification). Module 3 shows both.
- Graham's own definition says an "adequate" return. Secondary summaries say "satisfactory", and the course uses "adequate".

- Bogle's dollar cost illustration is his; we did not see the growth assumptions behind it.
- Graham's 1965 and 1972 earnings-yield figures are as quoted in the book's notes, not our calculation of market data.
- Birth and death years for Markowitz, Bogle and Graham come from Wikipedia or a news headline, not a primary record. Fisher's and Bachelier's years come from the Internet Archive catalogue.
- No UK fund-level evidence was read. The SPIVA figures are US only.
