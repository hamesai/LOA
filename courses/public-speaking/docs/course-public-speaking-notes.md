# Public speaking fundamentals: source notes

Built 11/10/2026 for beginners, general fundamentals, UK and Ireland assumed.

**Read in full:** Stand Up, Speak Out: The Practice and Ethics of Public Speaking (LibreTexts, CC BY-NC-SA 4.0), chapters 1 to 17 as fetched. That is 1.2 and 1.3; 2.2 and 2.3; 3.2 to 3.5; 4.2 to 4.6; 5.2 to 5.5; 6.2 to 6.5; 7.1 to 7.3; 8.1 to 8.3; 9.1 to 9.3; 10.1 to 10.4; 11.1 to 11.3; 12.1 and 12.2; 13.1 to 13.3; 14.1 to 14.4; 15.1 to 15.4; 16.1 and 16.2; 17.1 to 17.3. The NHS pages "Social anxiety (social phobia)" and the NHS breathing exercise were also read in full.

**Read in part:** The sample-speech bodies in chapter 6, the APA and MLA tables in chapter 7, and the sample introduction, body and conclusion analyses in chapters 9 to 11 were skimmed or used only for structure. The 17.x chapter fetched only sections 17.1 to 17.3.

**Not read:** Chapter 2.4 (Free Speech, US First Amendment) was not used. The chapter 19 appendix ("Your first speech") was not read. Chapter 18 was not fetched. The Nicolet "Exploring Public Speaking" PDF returned 403 and was not used. No UK or Irish guidance on free speech, defamation, discrimination, elections or charity fundraising was read.

**Source disagreements and cautions:**
- Mehrabian's 55-38-7 figures are repeated widely; the textbook itself notes they are misinterpreted. The course says not to use them as a rule.
- The "83 percent of learning is visual" and "10, 35, 65 percent recall" figures are quoted by the textbook without method. We did not check the originals, and the course flags them.
- The textbook's claim that slide design contributed to the Columbia disaster is Tufte's view as reported; it is not verified here.
- Monroe's motivated sequence is popular, but the text itself says the only experiment did not find it more persuasive.

**Limits:** The textbook is US-centred and dated 2013, so its news, political, legal and inclusive-language examples are American and may be out of date. The course marks UK and Irish adaptations as ours. Numbers marked "illustrative" are invented for teaching. YouTube, Google Images and Scholar links are search links whose results were not opened. This is not medical or legal advice.
