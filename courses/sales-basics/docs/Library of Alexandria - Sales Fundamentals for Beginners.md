## Module 1: What selling is: the seven steps and how people buy

Prerequisites: None. Time: 3 h.

### Core idea
Selling follows seven steps: prospect and qualify, preapproach, approach, presentation, objections, close and follow-up. People buy for needs and wants and, in business, as part of a buying group, with emotion as well as logic. FAB turns a feature into a benefit for this customer.

### Tutorial

1. Learn the seven steps and the buyer's matching steps.
2. Tell consumer (B2C) from business (B2B) buying and name the roles in a buying group.
3. Write a feature, advantage and benefit for an offer.

Worked example: A restaurant oven sale walks through all seven steps: a qualifying call, a researched visit, a trial lease for an objection, a close with a discount deadline and a follow-up call.

### Challenges

**C1 (warm-up).** List the seven steps in order and say which stage of the buyer's process each matches.
- Answer key: Prospect and qualify (need awareness), preapproach, approach, presentation (evaluation), objections, close (purchase), follow-up (implementation).
- Self-check: Compare your list with the table in Lesson 1.

**C2 (core).** Write a FAB statement for something you own, then rewrite the benefit for a different buyer.
- Answer key: Same feature and advantage, two different benefits tied to each buyer's motivation.
- Self-check: Does each benefit say what it means for that person, not just what the product does?

**C3 (stretch).** Sketch the buying group for a school buying 30 laptops. Name each role and say who sits at the power level.
- Answer key: Users (teachers, pupils), initiator (IT or head of department), influencers (IT, finance), decision maker (head teacher or governors, or the person controlling the budget).
- Self-check: Is there a decision maker and a gatekeeper named?

### Common mistakes

- Pitching before qualifying → find out if there is a need first.
- Selling features only → add the benefit for this customer.

## Module 2: Finding and qualifying prospects

Prerequisites: Module 1. Time: 3 h.

### Core idea
Prospecting never stops. A lead is a potential buyer; a prospect is a qualified lead. Choose sources that fit your ideal customer, qualify on need, authority, resources, willingness and access, and keep records.

### Tutorial

1. Write an ideal customer profile.
2. Choose prospecting sources and check the rules.
3. Qualify leads and rank them hot, warm or cold.

Worked example: 200 leads to 80 prospects is 40%; 80 to 40 presentations is 50%; 8 customers from 40 presentations is 20% and 4% of leads.

### Challenges

**C1 (warm-up).** A firm collects 150 leads, qualifies 45, presents to 30 and wins 6. Find each conversion rate.
- Answer key: 45 ÷ 150 = 30%; 30 ÷ 45 = 66.7%; 6 ÷ 30 = 20% of presentations; 6 ÷ 150 = 4% of leads.
- Self-check: Check each division.

**C2 (core).** Answer the five qualifying questions for three leads and rank them hot, warm or cold.
- Answer key: A table with a yes, no or unknown for need, authority, resources, willingness and access, and a rank for each lead.
- Self-check: Is each unknown turned into a question to ask?

**C3 (stretch).** Write an ideal customer profile and a source plan with one rule check for each cold source.
- Answer key: Three qualities, two problems solved, one fit point; sources with TPS, CTPS or consent notes.
- Self-check: Does each cold source name the rule you must check?

### Common mistakes

- Skipping qualification → ask the five questions.
- Treating stale contact data as current → date every record.

### Review

R1. What is a prospect? Answer: A lead qualified as ready, willing and able to buy.

### Resources

- [The Power of Selling (open textbook)](https://biz.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Marketing/The_Power_of_Selling) (Book, Beginner, Verified). Open textbook; chapters 4 and 6 to 12 were read (job-search sections skipped).

## Module 3: Preparing and opening the conversation

Prerequisites: Modules 1 and 2. Time: 3 h.

### Core idea
Prepare before you contact: research the company, customers, buying situation and contact; write a SMART objective and benefit statements; then open with the six Cs, a short opening and a permission question.

### Tutorial

1. Complete a precall worksheet.
2. Write a SMART objective and two benefit statements.
3. Script a 20-second opening and choose an approach.

Worked example: Opening: name, a reference point from research, a general benefit and 'Is this a good time?'.

### Challenges

**C1 (warm-up).** Rewrite 'find out more about their business' as a SMART objective.
- Answer key: A goal such as: by the end of the call, learn the current supplier, the main issue and two priorities, and agree a follow-up date by Friday.
- Self-check: Is it specific, measurable, actionable, realistic and time-bound?

**C2 (core).** Write a 20-second phone opening with a permission question.
- Answer key: Name, company, a research reference, a general benefit and 'Is this a good time?'.
- Self-check: Time it aloud: under 20 seconds.

**C3 (stretch).** Complete a precall sheet for a real or realistic prospect with a general and a specific benefit statement and a lawful-contact check.
- Answer key: All sections filled; benefit statements show a need; the contact check names TPS or CTPS or consent.
- Self-check: Could another person make the call from your sheet?

### Common mistakes

- Pitching in the opening → ask permission and give a reason.
- Using a template email → personalise it.

### Review

R1. Name the five qualifying questions. Answer: Need, authority, resources, willingness and access.
R2. What is FAB? Answer: Feature, advantage, benefit.

## Module 4: Presenting: questions, value and proposals

Prerequisites: Modules 1 to 3. Time: 3 h.

### Core idea
Presenting is mostly listening. Prepare logistics and rehearse; use SPIN questions to uncover needs; follow five presentation steps; show value with cost-benefit and ROI; and write customer-specific proposals.

### Tutorial

1. Prepare logistics and rehearse.
2. Ask Situation, Problem, Implication and Need-payoff questions.
3. Quantify value honestly.

Worked example: Dishwasher: $3,000 cost, $1,000 a year saving, five years: benefit $2,000, ROI about 67%, break-even at three years.

### Challenges

**C1 (warm-up).** Write one SPIN question of each type for something you could sell.
- Answer key: Four questions in the order S, P, I, N, each of the right type.
- Self-check: Is the implication question about consequences?

**C2 (core).** A purchase costs £5,000 and saves £1,800 a year for 4 years. Find net benefit, ROI and break-even.
- Answer key: Savings £7,200; net benefit £2,200; ROI 44%; break-even 5,000 ÷ 1,800 = about 2.8 years.
- Self-check: Check ROI = 2,200 ÷ 5,000 × 100.

**C3 (stretch).** Outline a 10-minute presentation with five steps, ten or fewer slides and one demonstration.
- Answer key: A timed outline with rapport, general benefit, specific benefit, presentation and close/recap.
- Self-check: Does the specific benefit tie to a stated need?

### Common mistakes

- Listing advantages instead of benefits → tie to the stated need.
- Raising price first → show value, but state price clearly.

### Review

R1. What is a trial close? Answer: A question asking for an opinion rather than a decision.
R2. What should a SMART objective be? Answer: Specific, measurable, actionable, realistic, time-bound.

## Module 5: Objections, closing and follow-up

Prerequisites: Modules 1 to 4. Time: 3 h.

### Core idea
Objections are questions: pause, restate, ask, give evidence. Know the six types. Close by asking, using trial closes and buying signals. Negotiate on value with a floor and trade concessions, and follow up to earn repeat business.

### Tutorial

1. List likely objections and answers.
2. Choose a close and ask for a next step.
3. Plan a negotiation and a follow-up.

Worked example: List £1,200 a month, floor £1,000: give 10% off (£1,080) only for a 24-month term.

### Challenges

**C1 (warm-up).** Classify each: 'Your price is higher', 'We're happy with our supplier', 'Never heard of you', 'I need to think about it', 'We have no budget', 'It might not work as well as X'.
- Answer key: Price, already satisfied (need), source, stall, money, product.
- Self-check: Check against the six-type table.

**C2 (core).** List £2,400 a year with a floor of £2,000. The buyer asks for 15% off. What is the price, is it above the floor, and what do you ask for in return?
- Answer key: 15% of 2,400 = 360, price £2,040, above the floor; ask for a longer term, a bigger order or faster payment.
- Self-check: Never concede without getting something.

**C3 (stretch).** Write two objection replies (restate, question, evidence) and a follow-up plan with dates.
- Answer key: Replies follow the loop; the plan has a thank-you, a check-in date and a referral request.
- Self-check: Are all claims provable?

### Common mistakes

- Arguing → pause and restate.
- Discounting at the first ask → get a concession back.

### Review

R1. What does the Irish source say about false limited offers? Answer: They are a banned unfair practice.
R2. What is an ROI? Answer: Net benefit divided by cost, times 100.

## Module 6: Selling fairly: ethics and the law (UK and Ireland)

Prerequisites: Modules 1 to 5. Time: 3 h.

### Core idea
Sell honestly and lawfully. Disclose what matters, keep your word, avoid misleading, aggressive or banned practices, and check consent and TPS or CTPS rules before calling or emailing. Keep respectful records.

### Tutorial

1. Apply an ethics check.
2. Check a contact against the UK rules.
3. Write your own sales code and contact rules.

Worked example: A sole trader with no relationship needs consent for a marketing email; a company's general inbox does not, but you must say who you are and offer an opt-out.

### Challenges

**C1 (warm-up).** For each, say whether a marketing email is allowed in the UK: a sole trader with no relationship; a company's info@ address; a customer who bought last week and was offered an opt-out; a bought-in list of individuals.
- Answer key: Needs consent; allowed with who-you-are and opt-out; soft opt-in may allow; not allowed without consent (soft opt-in does not apply to bought-in lists).
- Self-check: Check against the ICO table in Lesson 3.

**C2 (core).** Rewrite a pushy script that says 'only today' and 'you can't afford to wait'.
- Answer key: A version with a true deadline, written price and terms, and no pressure.
- Self-check: Is any deadline real and any claim provable?

**C3 (stretch).** Write a five-line personal sales code and five contact rules, and note which rules apply to B2C versus B2B in Ireland.
- Answer key: Code on honesty, disclosure, promises, gifts and objections; contact rules on TPS or CTPS, opt-outs and records; note that the Irish consumer rules cover B2C only.
- Self-check: Does it name the source of each rule?

### Common mistakes

- Assuming business emails need no care → named work emails are personal data.
- Ignoring an opt-out → stop and record.

### Review

R1. What is the SPIN sequence? Answer: Situation, Problem, Implication, Need-payoff.
R2. What is a gatekeeper? Answer: A person who controls access to the decision maker.

## Capstone: Plan and role-play a first sales conversation

Plan and role-play a first sales conversation: qualify, prepare, open honestly, question with SPIN, handle objections, ask for a next step, and stay within the rules.

**Deliverable:** Complete the one-page call plan.; Role-play the call with a partner and score it.; Write a short review..

**Rubric**

- Qualify and prepare: Show an ideal customer profile, five qualifying answers, sourced research, a SMART objective and benefit statements. Passing result: All present and sourced.
- Question, present and handle objections: Show SPIN questions, a FAB benefit, honest numbers and replies to two objections. Passing result: All present; numbers show inputs.
- Close, follow up and stay lawful: Show a trial close, a next step, a follow-up plan and a contact and claims check. Passing result: All present; no misleading or pressure claims.

**Pass condition:** All three rubric rows are met and the review is written.
