# Library of Alexandria: Vintage reselling business (UK)

Level: Beginner. Created 13/10/2026.

Start an online business reselling vintage goods: decide your legal status, source at prices that leave a margin, date and grade items honestly, sell lawfully, list and price across platforms, and keep records and tax right.

## Module 1: Is this a business? The model, the margin and the legal line

Prerequisites: None. Time: 3 h.

### Core idea
A vintage resale business earns only what is left after every step of the loop. Track margin on the selling price, say only what you can prove about age, and decide honestly whether you are a private seller or a trader, because the answer shapes the law, tax and platform rules that apply to you.

### Tutorial

1. Describe the resale loop and calculate margin and markup.
2. Score yourself against the Trading Standards indicators of being in business.
3. List the four consequences of being a trader.

Worked example: Bought £8, sold £32: profit £24, markup 300%, margin 75% before fees.

### Challenges

**C1 (warm-up).** You buy a coat for £12 and sell it for £30. Work out profit, markup and margin.
- Answer key: Profit £18; markup 18 ÷ 12 = 150%; margin 18 ÷ 30 = 60%.
- Self-check: Is margin divided by the selling price?

**C2 (core).** Score two sellers against the six indicators: buy to resell; make or repair; frequent or large quantity; several channels; rely on the income; present as a business.
- Answer key: A table with yes or no for each indicator and a short conclusion for each seller. Sam, who buys at car boots weekly, scores most or all.
- Self-check: Does each yes cite the facts about the seller?

**C3 (stretch).** Classify five scenarios as private seller, trader or unclear, and say what extra fact would settle each unclear one.
- Answer key: Five classifications with reasons. No single factor, such as having a job or not being VAT registered, settles a case.
- Self-check: Did you avoid treating employment or VAT status as decisive?

### Common mistakes

- Confusing markup with margin → divide profit by the selling price.
- Calling an item vintage without evidence → say vintage style or second-hand.
- Treating a job or lack of VAT registration as proof you are not in business → neither is decisive.

## Module 2: Sourcing: where to buy and the most you can pay

Prerequisites: Module 1. Time: 3 h.

### Core idea
Buy to a ceiling, not to a feeling. Work back from the expected sold price, take off fees, postage and packaging, and subtract the profit you need. Keep a buying log, know your rights at auction and measure sell-through.

### Tutorial

1. Choose a channel and visit it.
2. Work out the price ceiling for each candidate item.
3. Record each purchase and track sell-through.

Worked example: Expected sale £40 plus £6 postage; fees £5.58; postage £6.50; packing £1.20; target profit £15 gives a ceiling of £17.73.

### Challenges

**C1 (warm-up).** Expected sale £35 plus £5 postage charged. Fees: 10.9% of the total, a £0.40 order fee and 0.35% of the total. You pay £5.20 postage and £0.80 packing. Target profit £10. Find the ceiling.
- Answer key: Total £40; fees 4.36 + 0.40 + 0.14 = £4.90; net = 40 − 4.90 − 5.20 − 0.80 = £29.10; ceiling = £19.10.
- Self-check: Did you subtract your target profit last?

**C2 (core).** You listed 25 items and 17 sold in 60 days. Find the sell-through rate and say what it suggests.
- Answer key: 17 ÷ 25 = 68%. It suggests prices and sourcing are working, but check which items are slow.
- Self-check: Did you divide by items listed?

**C3 (stretch).** Visit two channels and record five items at each with asking price, expected sold price and a ceiling.
- Answer key: A log of ten items, each with a source note and a ceiling worked from sold prices.
- Self-check: Does every row show where the price evidence came from?

### Common mistakes

- Paying on feeling rather than a worked ceiling → calculate it before you handle the item.
- Using asking prices as evidence → use sold prices.
- Assuming you have consumer rights at a public auction you can attend → check the terms.

### Review

R1. Which is better evidence of an item's value: an asking price or a sold price? Answer: A sold price.
R2. Why does being in business matter for your platform account? Answer: Free or zero-fee tiers are meant for private sellers, and resellers need the business account.

### Resources

- [Second-hand upholstered furniture: fire safety](https://www.businesscompanion.info/en/quick-guides/product-safety/second-hand-upholstered-furniture-fire-safety) (Reference, Beginner, Verified). Pre-1950 exclusion, labels, disposal.
- [Second-hand electrical goods](https://www.businesscompanion.info/en/quick-guides/product-safety/second-hand-electrical-goods) (Reference, Beginner, Verified). Sellers' responsibilities; plugs; electric blankets.

## Module 3: Knowing what you hold: dating, condition and authenticity

Prerequisites: Modules 1–2. Time: 3 h.

### Core idea
Say only what you can support. Build a date from checkable evidence, grade condition on one scale, list every flaw with a photograph, and describe marked or branded items by what they say rather than what you hope they are.

### Tutorial

1. Collect clues and rank them on the evidence ladder.
2. Grade condition and write a flaw-by-flaw statement.
3. Follow the authenticity routine and record your confidence.

Worked example: A glass dish with a maker's mark, a mould seam and a decimal price sticker supports 'moulded glass dish, made between 1971 and 1985', with each clue stated.

### Challenges

**C1 (warm-up).** Rank these six clues from strongest to weakest: a smell of age; a dated receipt naming the item; a seller's story; a label design that matches a dated example; a hallmark; the style of the piece.
- Answer key: Strongest: dated receipt, hallmark. Medium: label design. Weakest: style, seller's story, smell.
- Self-check: Did you place documents and hallmarks above style and smell?

**C2 (core).** A jug is 17 cm tall, has a hairline crack about 2 cm inside the handle join, fine crazing and no chips, and you filled it with water and it did not leak. Write the condition statement.
- Answer key: A statement giving size, grade, the crack with size and position, the crazing, no chips, the leak test and any limit on use.
- Self-check: Does it say where each flaw is and what you tested?

**C3 (stretch).** Pick a branded item and follow the five-step authenticity routine. Write your conclusion and your confidence.
- Answer key: Photographs of marks, a named reference, a conclusion, a confidence level and, if unsure, wording such as 'stamped with the name of'.
- Self-check: Does the listing wording match your confidence?

### Common mistakes

- Treating style or smell as proof of age → rank evidence and state confidence.
- Hiding or editing out a flaw → photograph and describe it.
- Claiming 'genuine' without evidence → describe what the marks say.

### Review

R1. What price do you work the ceiling back from? Answer: The expected sold price, less fees, postage, packaging and target profit.

### Resources

- [Consumer Rights Act 2015, Explanatory Notes](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2015/15/notes/division/3/1/data.htm) (Reference, Intermediate, Verified). Excerpt read.
- [Hallmarking](https://www.businesscompanion.info/en/quick-guides/miscellaneous/hallmarking) (Reference, Beginner, Verified). Weight exemptions, dealers' notice, descriptions.
- [Deal in items made of or containing ivory](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/dealing-in-items-containing-ivory-or-made-of-ivory) (Reference, Beginner, Verified). What counts as dealing; penalties.
- [Etsy Reselling Guide 2026 (secondary)](https://www.underpriced.app/blog/etsy-reselling-guide-2026) (Reference, Beginner, Verified). Reports the 20-year vintage rule; check Etsy's own policy.

### Pass condition
Clues are ranked (C1), a condition statement is written (C2) and an authenticity note is made (C3).

## Module 4: Selling lawfully: restricted goods, safety and buyer rights

Prerequisites: Modules 1–3. Time: 3 h.

### Core idea
Some older goods cannot lawfully be sold by a business, or only with conditions. Ivory needs an exemption, precious metals above set weights need a hallmark, post-1949 upholstered furniture needs a label, and electrical goods must be safe. A trader owes buyers an accurate description and the 14-day cancellation right.

### Tutorial

1. Run the five gates before you list.
2. Apply the weight rules to precious-metal items.
3. Write a returns text and check the cancellation dates.

Worked example: Silver spoon 45 g unmarked: above 7.78 g, so do not sell as silver until advised. Silver ring 4 g: below 7.78 g, so the weight exemption may apply, but the description must be true.

### Challenges

**C1 (warm-up).** Which of these need a hallmark under the weight rules: a 6.5 g silver pendant, a 9 g silver pendant, a 0.8 g gold ring, a 0.6 g platinum charm?
- Answer key: 6.5 g silver: under 7.78 g, exempt. 9 g silver: needs a hallmark. 0.8 g gold: under 1 g, exempt. 0.6 g platinum: over 0.5 g, needs a hallmark.
- Self-check: Did you compare each weight with the threshold for its metal?

**C2 (core).** Decide for each: a 1935 upholstered chair, a 1980 sofa with no label, a lamp with a cracked cable, an electric blanket.
- Answer key: 1935 chair: before 1950, excluded. 1980 sofa with no label: do not sell without expert advice. Lamp: not as working; repair by a competent person or sell as for parts with a warning. Electric blanket: do not sell second-hand.
- Self-check: Did you give a reason for each decision?

**C3 (stretch).** A parcel is delivered on 20/10/2026. By what date can a consumer cancel? What if you never told them of the right?
- Answer key: 14 days after delivery is 03/11/2026. If never told, up to 12 months.
- Self-check: Did you count days across the month end?

### Common mistakes

- Listing a carved item that might be ivory → stop and confirm first.
- Calling a metal item gold or silver without testing → use a true description.
- Selling untested mains electrical items as working → do not, or sell for parts with a warning.

### Review

R1. What is the maximum penalty for dealing in ivory without an exemption? Answer: A fine of up to £250,000 or up to five years' imprisonment.

## Module 5: Platforms, listings and real profit

Prerequisites: Modules 1–4. Time: 3 h.

### Core idea
Choose a platform by the fees on its own page and by the buyers it brings. Photograph the whole item, its marks and every flaw. Price from sold evidence and measure real profit, including postage, packaging, fees and your time.

### Tutorial

1. Compare two platforms using their fee pages.
2. Re-photograph and re-list one item with the shot list.
3. Calculate real profit and profit per hour.

Worked example: Bought £22; sold £60 plus £7 postage; fees £7.94; postage £7.00; packing £1.50; profit £28.56 (42.6% margin).

### Challenges

**C1 (warm-up).** Bought for £9. Sells for £28 plus £4 postage charged (total £32). Fees: 10.9% of the total, £0.40 order fee, 0.35% of the total. You pay £4.50 postage and £0.60 packing. Find profit and margin.
- Answer key: Fees = 3.488 + 0.40 + 0.112 = £4.00. Profit = 32 − 4.00 − 4.50 − 0.60 − 9 = £13.90. Margin = 13.90 ÷ 32 = 43.4%.
- Self-check: Did you take the cost of the item off last?

**C2 (core).** For one item, calculate net proceeds on two platforms using their own fee pages, and write the page addresses and dates.
- Answer key: Two worked profit lines, with fee page addresses and dates.
- Self-check: Are the fee rules quoted from the pages you cite?

**C3 (stretch).** Write a 30-day markdown rule. State the cut, the new profit and the lowest price you will accept.
- Answer key: A rule stating the cut, a recalculated profit and a floor price.
- Self-check: Is the new price still above the floor?

### Common mistakes

- Assuming free tiers apply to a reseller → check the professional seller rules.
- Quoting fees without a date → record the page and date.
- Ignoring your time → compute profit per hour.

### Review

R1. Is margin divided by the buying price or the selling price? Answer: The selling price.

## Module 6: Shipping, returns, records and tax

Prerequisites: Modules 1–5. Time: 3 h.

### Core idea
Pack for the journey, price postage from the weighed parcel, handle returns with evidence and keep the records that tax needs. Gross trading income over £1,000 means registering for Self Assessment, and a reseller's real costs are often higher than the £1,000 allowance.

### Tutorial

1. Pack and weigh one fragile item.
2. Create a returns log and read the reason codes.
3. Work out allowance versus real costs.

Worked example: Sales £2,400, costs £1,900: expenses give taxable profit £500; allowance gives £1,400.

### Challenges

**C1 (warm-up).** A clock sells for £48 and the buyer pays £5 postage. You pay £9.10 postage and £2.40 packing. How much do you lose on shipping?
- Answer key: Cost 9.10 + 2.40 = £11.50; buyer paid £5.00; gap £6.50.
- Self-check: Did you include the packing?

**C2 (core).** You made 60 sales and had 9 returns: 5 damaged in post, 2 not as described, 2 changed mind. Find the return rate and the biggest cause.
- Answer key: 9 ÷ 60 = 15%. Biggest cause: damaged in post (5 of 9). Improve packing and carrier cover first.
- Self-check: Did you divide by sales?

**C3 (stretch).** Gross sales £3,100. Stock £1,900; fees £450; postage and packing £300. Compare allowance with real costs.
- Answer key: Costs £2,650; profit £450. Allowance: 3,100 − 1,000 = £2,100. Real costs give £1,650 less taxable profit.
- Self-check: Did you apply one method, not both?

### Common mistakes

- Guessing postage → weigh and measure first.
- Claiming the allowance and expenses together → choose one.
- Not registering when gross income passes £1,000 → register for Self Assessment.

### Review

R1. What does gross trading income over £1,000 require? Answer: Registering for Self Assessment.

## Capstone: Build a launch pack for your first five items

Build a launch pack for your first five items: status, buying, evidence, compliance and money. Defend every number and claim with a source or a worked calculation.

**Deliverable:** Complete the five sections of the launch pack.; Check the pack from the buyer, officer and inspector views.; Write a short review and a next step..

**Rubric**

- Status and buying: Decide your status with indicators, and show five items each with a worked ceiling and a source record. Passing result: Status reasoned; five ceilings worked from sold prices; sources recorded.
- Evidence and compliance: Show a dating note, condition statement and gate check for each item. Passing result: All present; no unsupported claim; restricted and safety gates cleared or the item dropped.
- Money and records: Show a profit table with an hourly rate, the allowance versus real-costs comparison and a records plan. Passing result: Numbers check; one method chosen; records and tax gaps listed.

**Pass condition:** All three rubric rows are met and the review is written.
