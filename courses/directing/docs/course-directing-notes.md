# Directing for stage and screen: source notes

Built 11/10/2026 for an intermediate learner, covering stage first and screen second. Prices and rules in the sources are American unless stated.

**Read in full:**
- *Directing* (Nick Garcia, LibreTexts, CC BY 4.0, 2020): all nine chapters (what is a director, preparation, breaking down the script, design meetings, budgeting, casting, lessons in staging, the rehearsal process, the actor's process), as fetched from LibreTexts.
- *Contemporary World Cinema* (eCampusOntario Pressbooks, CC BY-NC-SA 4.0): the chapters on Mise-en-scène, Cinematography, Editing and Acting.
- *An Actor Prepares* (Konstantin Stanislavski, translated by Elizabeth Reynolds Hapgood, 1936): read in full from a copy of the text. The course cites the book, not the copy. The chapters used closely were Action, Units and Objectives, Emotion Memory, Communion, Adaptation, The Unbroken Line and The Super-Objective. The Imagination, Concentration, Relaxation, Faith and a Sense of Truth, Inner Motive Forces, Inner Creative State and Threshold of the Subconscious chapters were skimmed for context and are not cited.
- Britannica entries for Stanislavsky, Granville-Barker (search excerpt plus the print version), Eisenstein and Brook, fetched in full for the first, third and fourth.

**Read in part:**
- *Prefaces to Shakespeare*, volume VI (Batsford, 1974): Edward Moore's introduction and passages of the prefaces on *The Winter's Tale*, *Twelfth Night* and *A Midsummer Night's Dream* were read from the scanned text; the Macbeth preface, other prefaces and volumes I to V were not read. The scan has OCR errors.
- Britannica on Meyerhold and on biomechanics, Encyclopedia.com on Meyerhold, media-studies.com on Eisenstein's five methods, the RSC page on Brook's 1970 Dream and the Wikipedia page on *The Empty Space*: read as search excerpts, not fetched whole.

**Not read:**
- Meyerhold's own writings and Edward Braun's *Meyerhold on Theatre*. The terms otkaz, posyl and tochka come from a student study site and are marked unverified.
- Eisenstein's *Film Form*. The scan we found had no text layer. His five methods are taken from a secondary account.
- Brook's *The Empty Space* (the book). We use the Wikipedia article for the four modes and the definition of an empty space.
- Stanislavski's *Building a Character*, *Creating a Role* and *My Life in Art*. Tempo-rhythm and the late method of physical actions are not taught.
- The *Contemporary World Cinema* chapters on sound and genre; the video clips embedded in the book were not watched.
- The Harvard thesis on Granville-Barker's Savoy productions (listed as a search result only).
- UK and Irish law and guidance on performance rights, licensing, fire safety, safeguarding and performer safety.

**Source disagreements and cautions:**
- The film textbook calls Stanislavski's approach emotion-first and says actors let go of the text; *An Actor Prepares* tells the actor to attend to the given circumstances and forget feelings. The course shows them side by side and says which it follows. Britannica adds that Lee Strasberg's American Method is an adaptation and that Stanislavski kept changing his approach.
- Garcia's gender examples rely on stereotypes by his own admission, and his claim that over 70 percent of communication is non-verbal has no source. The course warns about both.
- Garcia's distance guide (feet between actors) is one teacher's rule of thumb. Distances vary by culture.
- The course's Britannica entries disagree about when biomechanics began (Hedda Gabler, 1906, or the early 1920s). The course says which it treats as which.
- Britannica criticises Eisenstein's *Strike* for imposing a symbol that falsified the scene.
- *An Actor Prepares* is a teaching narrative with an invented class and teacher, not a record of real rehearsals.

**Limits:** Garcia's budget figures, rights fees and process are for American educational and community theatre in 2020. All worked examples (the waiting-room scene, the budget, coverage times) are invented and marked illustrative. The mappings between stage and screen devices are our inference and are labelled. This is not legal or safety advice, and fights, intimate scenes and risky movement need qualified supervision.

## Book supplied by the learner (added 19/10/2026)

**The Art of Alfred Hitchcock, Donald Spoto, second revised edition, Anchor Books, 1992 (Knopf Doubleday ebook, 2012)** (46 chapters, about 96,000 words). The review is written up in `source-review-hitchcock.md`.
- Read in full: Chapter 20 Rope, Chapter 23 Strangers on a Train, Chapter 26 Rear Window, Chapter 33 Psycho, and the introduction to the centenary edition.
- Read in full on 19/10/2026 (45 of 46 parts; only the contents list was skipped). Review: `source-review-hitchcock.md`, Status: complete.
- Added to the course: planting, bookends and the controlling image (module 1), evidence from cast and crew on directing actors (module 2), the cost of long takes and lost preparation (module 3), and a worked model for designing a sequence (module 5). Spoto agrees with the earlier long-take case study, so nothing there was removed.
- Limits: Spoto is one critic's reading, and several points rest on Hitchcock's remarks as Spoto reports them. The accounts of Hitchcock and actors come from a critic and one leading actor, not from rehearsal records. Facts about the films (running of takes, camera tricks) are Spoto's account and were not checked against other sources.
