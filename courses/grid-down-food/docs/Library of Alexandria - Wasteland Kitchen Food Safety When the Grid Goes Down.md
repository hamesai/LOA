# Library of Alexandria: Wasteland Kitchen: Food Safety When the Grid Goes Down

Level: Beginner (new food business). Created 13/10/2026.

Run a safe frozen-meal catering business from a home kitchen, and keep it safe when the power or water fails: registration, hazard control, the freezer-failure clock, cooking and reheating, labelling and allergens, continuity, using UK, Irish, WHO and US guidance.

## Module 1: Five keys, key temperatures and who is at risk

Prerequisites: None. Time: 3 h.

### Core idea
Safe food rests on five WHO keys and a handful of temperatures. The numbers differ slightly between the UK, Ireland, the WHO and the US, and some people need stricter rules.

### Tutorial

1. Recite the five keys with a grid-down example each.
2. Compare fridge, freezer and cooking temperatures from four authorities.
3. Replace hand-washing with sanitiser or wipes when the tap is off.
4. List at-risk people and the stricter rule for them.

Worked example: 140 F converts to (140 - 32) x 5 / 9 = 60 C; the US fridge limit of 40 F is 4.4 C.

### Challenges

**C1 (warm-up).** Write the five keys and one grid-down example for each.
- Answer key: Keep clean, separate raw and cooked, cook thoroughly, safe temperatures, safe water and raw materials, each with a blackout example.
- Self-check: Compare with the table in lesson 1.

**C2 (core).** Convert 0 F, 40 F and 140 F to Celsius, then name which UK or Irish limit each is closest to.
- Answer key: -17.8 C (freezer), 4.4 C (fridge), 60 C (hot holding).
- Self-check: Recompute (F - 32) x 5 / 9.

**C3 (stretch).** Build a risk list for a household of five including a baby and a pregnant adult and write the rule for each.
- Answer key: At-risk people named; each has the 4-hour rule for chilled ready-to-eat food; baby has boiled-water or ready-to-use formula.
- Self-check: Check against the CMO and FSA lists.

### Common mistakes

- Assuming every authority gives the same number. State which source you are using.
- Skipping hand hygiene when the tap is off. Use sanitiser or wipes.
- Treating the 4-hour guide as a promise. It is an estimate.

## Module 2: The cold chain without power

Prerequisites: Module 1. Time: 3 h.

### Core idea
The fridge lasts about 4 hours, a full freezer about 48 and a half-full one about 24 if doors stay shut. You then eat in order of shelf life, decide thawed food by ice crystals and temperature, and audit what is left.

### Tutorial

1. Calculate the three clock times from the start of an outage.
2. Order a fridge from shortest to longest shelf life.
3. Decide refreeze or discard using FSIS, FSAI and CDC rules.
4. Audit a fridge and bin perishables above 40 F / 5 C for over 2 hours.

Worked example: Power off at 14:20: fridge mark 18:20, half-full freezer 14:20 next day, full freezer 14:20 two days later.

### Challenges

**C1 (warm-up).** The power failed at 09:15. Give the fridge, half-full and full freezer marks.
- Answer key: 13:15 same day; 09:15 next day; 09:15 two days later.
- Self-check: Add 4, 24 and 48 hours.

**C2 (core).** Sort ten foods into eat-first, then-next and later using the FSA order.
- Answer key: Chilled first, defrosted next, room-temperature perishable, cupboard last.
- Self-check: Compare with the figure in lesson 2.

**C3 (stretch).** A freezer has frozen chicken, soft ice cream, thawed but cold peas and warm thawed steak. Decide each and justify.
- Answer key: Chicken keep; ice cream discard; peas refreeze only if ice crystals and cold; steak discard.
- Self-check: Check the FSIS table.

### Common mistakes

- Opening the door to check. Use a thermometer.
- Tasting food to test it. Never taste.
- Refreezing food that has been warm over 2 hours.

### Review

R1. What is the WHO limit for cooked food at room temperature? (Module 1) Answer: 2 hours.
R2. Which people need stricter rules after 4 hours? (Module 1) Answer: Babies, young children, pregnant and breastfeeding women, older people and those with weakened immunity.

## Module 3: Safe water

Prerequisites: Modules 1-2. Time: 3 h.

### Core idea
Unsafe water must not be used for drinking, washing food, brushing teeth or formula. Boiling is the surest treatment for germs; bleach, tablets, filters and sunlight help when you cannot boil. Chemical, fuel or radioactive contamination cannot be treated at home.

### Tutorial

1. Identify the three advisories and what each means.
2. Boil water for the right time at your elevation.
3. Calculate bleach drops for a volume of water.
4. Use safe water for dishes, produce and infant formula.

Worked example: 10 litres of clear water with 5 to 9% bleach needs 2 x 10 = 20 drops; double if cloudy.

### Challenges

**C1 (warm-up).** State what to do for a boil water advisory and for a do-not-drink advisory.
- Answer key: Boil tap water for 1 minute or use bottled; do-not-drink means use bottled water as boiling does not help.
- Self-check: Compare with the CDC table.

**C2 (core).** Calculate bleach for 6 litres of cloudy water using 5 to 9% bleach, and the boil batches in a 3-litre pot.
- Answer key: 2 x 2 x 6 = 24 drops; 2 batches.
- Self-check: Recompute: 2 drops x 2 (cloudy) x 6 = 24.

**C3 (stretch).** Write a one-page water plan for a household of four for 24 hours, including cooking and washing.
- Answer key: Sources, treatment method, uses, container cleanliness, infant formula plan.
- Self-check: Check every use is covered by safe water.

### Common mistakes

- Using radiator water.
- Skipping the 30-minute wait with bleach.
- Boiling water with fuel or chemicals in it.

### Review

R1. Convert 140 F to Celsius. (Module 1) Answer: 60 C.
R2. How long is a closed fridge safe in a power cut? (Module 2) Answer: About 4 hours.

### Resources

- [How to make water safe in an emergency](https://www.cdc.gov/water-emergency/about/index.html) (Reference, Beginner, Verified). Read in full.
- [Drinking water advisories: an overview](https://www.cdc.gov/water-emergency/about/drinking-water-advisories-an-overview.html) (Reference, Beginner, Verified). Read from search extract (near complete).

### Pass condition
You can boil, disinfect and plan water for a household, and name water you cannot treat.

## Module 4: Cooking off-grid

Prerequisites: Modules 1-3. Time: 3 h.

### Core idea
Electric cookers fail; gas may not. Camping stoves and barbecues are for outdoors only because of carbon monoxide. Cook food steaming hot, stews to 70 C, thaw safely, and never prepare food for others when sick.

### Tutorial

1. List heat sources and where to use each.
2. Explain the carbon monoxide risk and response.
3. Cook and reheat to steaming hot or 70 C.
4. Apply the two-hour table rule.

Worked example: Dinner served at 18:30: by 20:30 eat, chill or discard.

### Challenges

**C1 (warm-up).** State the rule for camping stoves and barbecues and why.
- Answer key: Outdoors only; carbon monoxide has no smell and can kill.
- Self-check: Compare with lesson 2.

**C2 (core).** Plan a thawing method for a frozen chicken that will be cooked tonight, with times.
- Answer key: Fridge overnight, or cold water bag, or microwave then cook at once; never at room temperature.
- Self-check: Check against NCHFP and FSAI.

**C3 (stretch).** Write a carbon monoxide response card for the household.
- Answer key: Leave, call emergency numbers, do not re-enter, seek medical help; symptoms include headache and nausea.
- Self-check: Check against the UKHSA steps.

### Common mistakes

- Cooking indoors on a stove.
- Leaving cooked food out over 2 hours.
- Cooking while ill for the family.

### Review

R1. How long can a closed fridge hold food safely? (Module 2) Answer: About 4 hours.
R2. How long should you boil clear water? (Module 3) Answer: 1 minute at a rolling boil; 3 minutes above 6,500 ft (2,000 m).

### Resources

- [Five keys to safer food (poster)](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/infographics-pdf/food-safety/five-keys-to-safer-food.pdf) (Reference, Beginner, Verified). Read in full.

Read in full. Written for a national outage scenario.

## Module 5: Storing and preserving safely

Prerequisites: Modules 1-4. Time: 3 h.

### Core idea
A shelf-stable stock reduces risk. Know the difference between use-by and best-before, store freezer food within star ratings, and never improvise canning: low-acid foods need a pressure canner and unknown jars go in the bin.

### Tutorial

1. Build and rotate a stock by date.
2. Use freezer star ratings and temperatures.
3. State the canning rule for low-acid foods.
4. Recognise spoilage and botulism risk signs.

Worked example: At 3,200 ft, boil home-canned low-acid food 10 + 3 = 13 minutes.

### Challenges

**C1 (warm-up).** Order four tins by best-before dates and state what first-in first-out means.
- Answer key: Earliest date first; new stock at the back.
- Self-check: Check dates in order.

**C2 (core).** Compute the boil time for home-canned vegetables at 1,500 ft and 5,200 ft.
- Answer key: 11 minutes; 15 minutes.
- Self-check: 10 + whole thousands.

**C3 (stretch).** Write a decision card for jars: bulging, leaking, unknown origin, dented sealed tin.
- Answer key: First three: throw out; dented sealed tin: examine and avoid if damaged or broken.
- Self-check: Check against CDC and FSAI.

### Common mistakes

- Using a boiling-water canner for low-acid foods.
- Tasting to test a jar.
- Leaving garlic oil beyond 4 days.

### Review

R1. What temperature should stews reach? (Module 4) Answer: 70 C, brought to boiling.
R2. How should boiled water be stored? (Module 3) Answer: In clean, sanitised containers with tight covers.

### Resources

- [Keeping food safe during an emergency](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/keeping-food-safe-during) (Reference, Beginner, Verified). Read in full, including the save-or-discard tables.

## Module 6: Floods, decisions and the 72-hour plan

Prerequisites: Modules 1-5. Time: 3 h.

### Core idea
Floods add contamination, so decisions must be fast and stricter. You learn what to bin, how to sanitise surfaces and sealed cans, ten key numbers, where to get official information, and how to assemble a 72-hour plan.

### Tutorial

1. Decide what to save or discard after floodwater.
2. Recall the ten key numbers.
3. Plan information sources and sharing.
4. Draft a 72-hour plan.

Worked example: A 10-litre sanitising bucket at 1 cup per 5 gallons needs about 126 mL of bleach.

### Challenges

**C1 (warm-up).** List five foods to discard after floodwater contact.
- Answer key: Perishables, food in cardboard, screw-cap containers, bulging cans, home-canned food.
- Self-check: Compare with lesson 1.

**C2 (core).** Make a one-page card of ten numbers with sources.
- Answer key: Includes 4h, 48h, 24h, 8C, 40F/2h, 2h, 70C, 1 min, 30 min, 4 days.
- Self-check: Check each against its source.

**C3 (stretch).** Draft a 72-hour skeleton for your household.
- Answer key: Hours 0-4, 4-24, 24-72 each with food, water and checks.
- Self-check: Every row cites a rule.

### Common mistakes

- Salvaging cardboard-packed food from floodwater.
- Sharing unsafe food.
- Relying on phones for updates.

### Review

R1. Name carbon monoxide symptoms. (Module 4) Answer: Headache, tiredness, difficulty thinking, feeling sick.
R2. When is a jar thrown out? (Module 5) Answer: If bulging, leaking, damaged, spurting, discoloured, mouldy, smelly, or if the canning method is unknown.

## Capstone: Write a 72-hour continuity and food-safety plan for a frozen-product home catering business

Write a 72-hour grid-down continuity and food-safety plan for a frozen-product home catering business: registration, hazard control for three items, freezer-failure plan, reheating tests, labels and allergens, dispatch and customer messages, all with sources and honest labels on inference.

**Deliverable:** Gather the business case and the FSA sources.; Write the registration, hazard-control, freezer-failure and reheating sections.; Write labels, allergens, dispatch rules and customer messages, then score against both rubrics..

**Rubric**

- Registration and hazards: Show registration and permissions with sources, and a hazard-control table for three menu items with limits. Passing result: 28 days; 70 degrees for 2 minutes; chilled 8 degrees or below; failure actions.
- Freezer and reheating: Show a freezer-failure plan with times, a log and an illustrative value at risk, and a reheating test record. Passing result: FSA household estimates cited and marked as estimates; steaming hot throughout; reheat once.
- Labels, customers and honesty: Show a label with allergens emphasised, a distance-selling note, flags for alcohol and claims, dispatch rules, and inference labelled. Passing result: Allergen information before purchase and at delivery; licensing and claims flagged; inference labelled.

**Pass condition:** Rubric score of at least 8 out of 10 with no zeros and no safety failures.

**Not read**

**Where sources differ (shown side by side in the lessons)**
- Fridge limit: UK under 8 C in a power cut (FSA), Ireland 0 to 5 C (FSAI), US 40 F (FSIS).
- Milk, yoghurt and eggs after the fridge fails: FSIS discards perishables above 40 F for over 2 hours; the UK CMO script for a long national outage allows some for days if they look and smell normal. The course says to use the stricter rule when other safe food exists, and always for at-risk people. That recommendation is our reading.
- Refreezing: FSAI says do not refreeze defrosted food unless first cooked above 75 C; FSIS and CDC allow refreezing while ice crystals remain.

**Our inference or framing (labelled in the lessons)**
- Scaling arithmetic (for example bleach per litre from the CDC per-gallon ratio), the "stricter rule" advice, practical tips such as filling bottles before a warning, and the 72-hour skeleton table.

**Illustrative numbers**
All households, dates and times used in examples are invented.
