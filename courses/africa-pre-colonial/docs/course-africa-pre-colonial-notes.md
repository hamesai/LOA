# Pre-Colonial Africa: how far the sources were read

Read this before relying on any lesson. The course is written from a finished working library (Library of Alexandria: Pre-Colonial Africa, 30/10/2026), which was itself built from public sources and the author's knowledge of the scholarship, not from books read in full. This build added a small number of web pages, listed below, all opened on 30/10/2026. Pages were fetched as excerpts; none of them is a full reading of a book.

## Sources
- part | Working library: Pre-Colonial Africa (to c. 1880) | all | The structure, claims, tags and challenges of every module come from it. It is a pointer to the literature, not a primary source. Its link check (30/10/2026) was by automated request; two museum sites could not be opened.
- full | Slave Voyages: Estimates, Trans-Atlantic Slave Trade | 8 | The whole table of embarked people by 50-year period and region was read. All shares in the lessons are our arithmetic, checked in code. The disembarked total of about 10.7 million is taken from the working library and was not re-checked.

## Lecturers
- Dossiers for Jan Vansina, Ibn Khaldun, Ibn Battuta, Samuel Johnson and Olaudah Equiano are built from the pages above, with confidence tag partial. None of their own works was read. Voiced lines are simulated, written for teaching and not quotations.

## Not read, or not verified
- Whether the Rhodes-era, Zulu, Dahomey and Kongo details in the working library match the specialist books. They were taken as written.
- Ibn Battuta's visit to Kilwa: Wikipedia says 1330 and the working library says 1331. The lessons give about 1330 to 1331 and note that his chronology is uncertain.
- The Berlin Conference attendance: the working library says twelve European states plus the Ottoman Empire and the United States; the Wikipedia excerpt says thirteen European nations and the United States. Both total fourteen, and the lessons say fourteen states.
- Samuel Johnson's stated reasons for writing are from a biography and the preface excerpt, not from the whole book.
- Equiano's birth year (c. 1745 versus about 1747 from the baptism record) is approximate.
- Restitution of the Benin objects: marked check current in the lessons.
- Every link marked unverified in the working library (Smithsonian National Museum of African Art; the British Museum collection) is left out of the resources.
- The 10.7 million disembarked figure and the one-in-seven middle passage ratio rest on the working library.
- The lecturers' definitions and the barrier-aid answers (shared/barriers) were written from the lessons and not checked against further sources.
- The course has no per-lesson lecturer notes (shared/course-lecturer-notes.js).
- The Post-Colonial Africa course is separate and was not touched.
