Run on 01/11/2026. Nothing below has been downloaded, ingested or read in full. Nothing enters the course until you choose it.

**How to read the Status column.** *full text available* means the link loaded and the whole text is offered openly. *metadata only* means the page loaded but I saw only its catalogue record or abstract. *excerpt* means a search excerpt was seen. None means read.

**Licence note.** Public-domain means the text is old enough to be out of copyright (Johnson 1921, Kebra Nagast translation 1932, Gibb translation 1929, Pory 1600 as reprinted 1896, Houdas 1900). The UNESCO volumes carry an open licence on the UNESCO Digital Library (the records for vol. IV and vol. VIII show CC BY-SA 3.0 IGO). Anything marked *paywalled* or *institutional login* is listed only so you know it exists; I would not use it unless you hold a legitimate copy.

## What the course already has, and where it is thinnest

The books you supplied (Reader, Rodney, Olivier, both Diop, Woodson) add popular survey, thesis and one-biography depth. Gaps found by reading the library and course notes:

- **African-authored and early outside primary texts** are cited but not held: Johnson (Yoruba), the Timbuktu chronicles, Ibn Battuta, Leo Africanus, the *Kebra Nagast*.
- **A peer-reviewed spine** for Modules 1–7 (the UNESCO *General History of Africa* is named in the library but not ingested).
- **Archaeology and genetics papers** for Nok, the Bantu expansion, and the Mapungubwe–Zimbabwe sequence, where the course states debates but cites mostly general books.
- **Thin regions:** Madagascar, the pre-camel Sahara, Egypt after Kush, Somalia and the Horn coast (named as omitted in Appendix C).

2. The Chirikure and Grollemund papers (short, free, and they target debates the course already tags **[CONTESTED]**).
3. Johnson, *Kebra Nagast*, and the Timbuktu chronicle (African-authored primary texts).
