The Creati ve Process 
How do you make something? Whether you are making a paper plane, a fried egg, or something much 
more complex, you are most likely following a process. 
When you make a film, or any other work of art, that process is not just technical—it’s also creative. 
Understanding the creative process is as essen tial as kn owing the technical steps needed to buil d 
whatever you have dreamed up. 
At the heart of the creative process lies the ability to ask questions about the world around you. How 
does it impact you? What is your unique perspective on it? How do you want to share that perspective? 
Answering these questions leads you on a journey of creation. 
The creator is you. 
So, what do you have to say? This question has a double meaning. Not only does it ask what resides 
in you that you could share with the world, but also what you must share with the world:

The Medi um 
Sharing experiences is a very human thing. Human beings are social creatures, and sharing information 
about the world and our feelings about it is baked into who we are as a species. 
We tell stories to each other for the benefit of the group. It is a fun damental way in which we make 
sense of the world around us and convey meaning to one another.

Here a re th ree basi c ca tegories of filmmakin g f or y ou to consi der. Ea ch ca tegory f ollows a se t of 
guidelines that will determine how you approach your film. 
 
Avant-Garde 
This is an experimental approach to filmmaking that intentionally breaks conventional narrative 
and visual rules to challenge an audience’s perceptions. 
Nonfiction 
This is the portrayal of real people or events and is often referred to as “documentary.” 
Fiction 
This is the fictional portrayal of invented characters and events. 
One can apply each of these approaches to the same story. This will inevitably change how that story is 
conveyed and how the audience interacts with it. 
Note: These approaches are not mutually exclusive. Avant-garde technique can find its way into nonfiction or 
fiction filmmaking as easily as nonfiction technique can show up in fiction or avant-garde films. The same is true for 
fiction filmmaking technique.
