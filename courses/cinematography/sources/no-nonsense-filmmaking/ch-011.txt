THE STAGES OF PRODUCTION 
In this chapter you will learn: 
• What the production process looks like from start to finish. 
A film does not begin on a screen. 
It begins as an idea, is developed in stages, and involves collaboration with numerous other people. 
These stages are similar, regardless of which category a film project falls into (fiction, nonfiction, 
experimental, or beyond). 
Development Development 
Your film begins with a thought bouncing around inside your head. It zooms 
from left to right and transforms into an idea searching for a way out. 
Eventually, it escapes onto a page, but it isn’t satisfied to stay there. 
Preproduction Preproduction 
With the help of some friends, you coax it forward, slowly but surely. 
Production Production 
Then, it leaps off the page and stands before a camera that captures it, one piece at a time. 
Post Production Post Production 
Those pieces are diligently put together, and the film starts to feel whole. 
Slowly, it can see, hear, and breathe. 
Distribution Distribution 
Then it begins to dance on its own on a giant screen, where it plays forever and ever for an audience that cheers and 
applauds and demands more. 
As a filmmaker, it is your job to shape your ideas as you enter each stage. You must 
be able to adapt to the unique set of logistics your film requires. Films may differ in 
terms of content and form, but the steps taken to bring them to the screen always 
follow the same five stages.
