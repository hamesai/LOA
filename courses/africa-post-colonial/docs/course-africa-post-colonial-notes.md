# Post-Colonial Africa: how far the sources were read

Read this before relying on any lesson. The course is written from a finished working library (Library of Alexandria: Post-Colonial Africa, 30/10/2026), which was built from public sources and the author's knowledge of the scholarship, and then deepened on 01/11/2026 with a source review of seven books you supplied (Hochschild, Reader, Rodney, Moyo, Garvey, Welsh, Blackmon). Each book's sections were read or skipped with a reason, as the cards below show. The web pages opened on 30/10/2026 are listed below.

## Book notes
- hochschild | Module 1 (Congo mechanics, the toll, the 1908 handover and 'why the Congo?'), with uses in 2, 3, 4 and 7. A popular narrative history, read in full. His toll of about 10 million is his own reckoning from a halving of a base nobody counted, so it stays [ESTIMATE, CONTESTED] beside Ndaywel (13 million, later lowered to 10 million) and Reader's different base. His explanations of Africa's troubles by strongmen and indigenous slavery in the afterword are interpretation and are not taught as fact. Several names and dates were checked only inside the book.
- reader | Modules 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 and 7. A popular survey with a strong ecology emphasis. 17 of 63 sections were read, covering the colonial, independence and South African chapters; the pre-colonial and ecological chapters were skipped on purpose. It is not a historian's monograph: the lessons use its facts and flag its slips (Berlin 'carved up' blurb, Congo population base of 10 million against 20 million, Pan-African Congress number, Cape franchise removal, Kenyatta as President in 1963, Mandela gaoled for treason, Rwanda identity cards in 1926). One earlier finding about Nigeria (state counts and oil revenue) was not found in the chapter cited and was not used.
- rodney | Modules 1, 2, 4 and 6. A self-declared political argument of 1972 (dependency school), presented as one position beside Moyo and mainstream economists. 7 of 14 sections were read; the pre-colonial chapters and indexes were skipped. Dates corrected rather than repeated: Brazzaville is 1944 not 1948, Chilembwe 1915 not 1917, Vy Vato 1913 not 1816. His nutrition claims are not taught as fact.
- moyo | Module 6. A contested 2009 argument with dated numbers. The PDF had no outline, so it was read in nine blocks of about twenty pages; text extraction was clean. The poverty claim (11 to 66 per cent) is untraceable in her own notes, her China figures are inconsistent, and several claims were overtaken by events. Current aid, poverty and remittance figures come from search results on 01/11/2026 and were not checked against the original publications.
- garvey | Module 3. A primary source in a partisan voice, read in full except front and back matter. Membership figures are his own claims. The critics' case (the Klan meeting, the Mississippi resolution, the Black Star Line conviction, the Liberia refusal) is shown beside his.
- welsh | Module 5, the main source. Read in full except the notes and bibliography. It corrected the Soweto toll (575 countrywide to 02/1977 in place of 176 and about 700), the 1913 and 1936 land figures, the founding name of the ANC, Mandela's arrest date and the Rivonia verdicts, and added the Tricameral Parliament, the debt standstill, CODESA's dates and the stalemate thesis. Welsh contradicts himself in places (Rivonia convictions, the 1973 Durban strike count, two sets of violence totals) and the lessons flag this.
- blackmon | Module 3 only. A US-focused history. 9 of 26 sections were read (front matter, introduction, chapters I and II, chapter XIII, chapter XVII and the epilogue); chapters III to XII and XIV to XVI and the notes were skipped as US-specific detail. Findings are limited and are used only as a documented contrast to the Willie Lynch hoax, which stays labelled a hoax.

## Not read, or not verified
- Disagreements between the working library and the pages we opened are shown in the lessons, not resolved: Sharpeville (69 killed against at least 91), Herero and Nama (about 65,000 of 80,000 against 40,000 to 80,000 and 10,000 Nama), Rwanda (the official 1,074,017 against about 507,000 and 500,000 to 600,000), OAU signatories (32 against 33), Adwa casualties, Mau Mau counts, and the Congo Free State (about 10 million against about half the population).
- The Willie Lynch lesson uses only what Cobb, the Baltimore Sun and Longreads report. We did not search an archive for the 1970 Black Liberation Library printing or check the word dates independently. A search for the letter's earliest printing is a task for a specialist.
- The statement that none of our three sources cites a contemporary record of a 1712 James River meeting is our reading of them.
- Mandela's 27 years in custody: resolved from Welsh, who gives his arrest on 05/08/1962, so 27 years and 6 months to 11/02/1990 (our arithmetic).
- The recent events in Module 7 (Sudan, South Sudan, the Sahel, Tunisia, Libya) were not re-checked and are marked check current.
- The lecturers' definitions and the barrier-aid answers (shared/barriers) were written from the lessons and not checked against further sources.
- The course has no per-lesson lecturer notes (shared/course-lecturer-notes.js).
- The Pre-Colonial Africa course is separate and was not touched.
- kit.py ship was not used: the earlier session reported that it stops at its gate because of open findings in the unrelated directing course. The steps were run by hand (check_course, cache bump, markdown library write, open) and the directing course was not touched.
- source_verify.py cannot match dates written DD/MM/YYYY, thousands printed with spaces (Welsh) or decimal commas (Welsh), or numbers printed as words (Hochschild's nineteen thousand). Every flagged sentence of this kind was checked by hand against the cited chapter; the remaining flags are dates.
- Welsh's chapters are cited by his chapter number (file ch-003 is his chapter 1), Reader's by book chapter number, and Moyo's by the 20-page block, because her PDF has no chapters.
- The Biafra paragraph uses only what Reader's chapter 55 says. Death counts for Biafra, the Derg and Rwanda are still those of the working library and web pages.
- Not read in this pass: Reader's pre-colonial chapters, Rodney's chapters II to V, the middle of Blackmon, and the notes and bibliographies of Welsh and Reader.
