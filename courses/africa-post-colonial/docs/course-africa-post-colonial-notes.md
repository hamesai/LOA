# Post-Colonial Africa: how far the sources were read

Read this before relying on any lesson. The course is written from a finished working library (Library of Alexandria: Post-Colonial Africa, 30/10/2026), which was built from public sources and the author's knowledge of the scholarship, not from books read in full. This build opened a small number of web pages on 30/10/2026, listed below. No book was read.

## Sources
- part | Working library: Post-Colonial Africa (c. 1880 to the present) | all | The structure, claims, tags and challenges of every module come from it. It is a pointer to the literature, not a primary source. Its link check (30/10/2026) was by automated request. Its recent-events claims (2020 to 2026) come from pages its builder read on the dates given; we did not reopen them.
- full | Jelani Cobb: Is Willie Lynch's Letter Real? (Jim Crow Museum, May 2004) | 3, cap | Read in full through the Internet Archive copy, because the live Ferris address returned only a redirect page. The anachronism dates (program 1837, indoctrination 1832, self-refueling 1811, outline 1759, lynching not before 1768) are Cobb's and were not re-checked in a dictionary.
- full | Baltimore Sun: In search of 'Willie' Lynch (22/02/1998) | 3 | Read in full through the Internet Archive copy. The article reports the 1970 Black Liberation Library printing, the St. Louis Black Pages, the University of Missouri posting, and comments from historians including Eric Foner and Gerald Horne.
- full | Longreads: The Enduring Legacy of the Willie Lynch Hoax (02/05/2018) | 3 | Read in full. It says the first posting was by a librarian at a University of Missouri library at Kansas City; the Baltimore Sun and the web address it links say St. Louis. The lesson reports the disagreement and does not settle it.

## Not read, or not verified
- Disagreements between the working library and the pages we opened are shown in the lessons, not resolved: Sharpeville (69 killed against at least 91), Herero and Nama (about 65,000 of 80,000 against 40,000 to 80,000 and 10,000 Nama), Rwanda (the official 1,074,017 against about 507,000 and 500,000 to 600,000), OAU signatories (32 against 33), Adwa casualties, Mau Mau counts, and the Congo Free State (about 10 million against about half the population).
- The Willie Lynch lesson uses only what Cobb, the Baltimore Sun and Longreads report. We did not search an archive for the 1970 Black Liberation Library printing or check the word dates independently. A search for the letter's earliest printing is a task for a specialist.
- The statement that none of our three sources cites a contemporary record of a 1712 James River meeting is our reading of them.
- Mandela's 27 years in custody: the lesson works 12/06/1964 to 11/02/1990 (25 years 8 months) and says the usual figure counts from his arrest in 1962, a date not in the library [check current].
- The recent events in Module 7 (Sudan, South Sudan, the Sahel, Tunisia, Libya) were not re-checked and are marked check current.
- The lecturers' definitions and the barrier-aid answers (shared/barriers) were written from the lessons and not checked against further sources.
- The course has no per-lesson lecturer notes (shared/course-lecturer-notes.js).
- The Pre-Colonial Africa course is separate and was not touched.
- kit.py ship was not used: the earlier session reported that it stops at its gate because of open findings in the unrelated directing course. The steps were run by hand (check_course, cache bump, markdown library write, open) and the directing course was not touched.
