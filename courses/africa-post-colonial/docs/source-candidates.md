**How to read the Status column.** *full text available* means the link loaded and the whole text is offered openly. *metadata only* means the page loaded but I saw only its catalogue record. *excerpt* means a search excerpt was seen. None means read.

**Licence note.** Government and UN reports are free to read; the licences differ, so check the reuse terms before quoting more than 15 words. *Public domain* means out of copyright. Items marked *institutional* or *paywalled* are listed only so you know they exist.

## What the course already has, and where it is thinnest

The books you supplied (Reader, Rodney, Hochschild, Moyo, Garvey, Welsh, Blackmon) are popular histories, a thesis, an argument against aid, a primary-speech collection, and a scholarly apparatus for apartheid. Gaps found:

- **Primary documents** are cited by name but not held: the Casement Report, Lumumba and Mandela speeches, the Arusha Declaration, the OAU Charter, Sankara's speeches, the Berg Report and the Lagos Plan.
- **Official inquiries** for contested numbers: the TRC (South Africa), the Carlsson report (UN, Rwanda), Kenya's TJRC (Mau Mau context), Human Rights Watch's Rwanda study.
- **No African-scholar spine:** the UNESCO *General History of Africa* vols. VII–IX (named in Module 1 resources but not ingested).
- **No data** for the economic and governance arguments in Modules 6 and 7 (Moyo, Rodney and adjustment claims rest on author figures).
- **Thin topics:** the Sahel jihadist conflict, Somalia, Zimbabwe after 1980, Nigeria after 1970, Angola and Mozambique (named in Appendix C).
